In Nigerian commerce, corporate executives often assume that once a company is incorporated, its legal existence is guaranteed forever.

Inside the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC), the reality is starkly different. Incorporation is an ongoing statutory contract. Under Section 692 of CAMA 2020, where a company fails to file annual returns for two consecutive years, the Commission possesses full legal authority to strike its name off the register and dissolve the company.

More than 100,000 Nigerian companies have been gazetted for strike-off. In this edition, we examine the three critical warning signs of CAC delinquency and explain how to restore active standing in 24 hours.

CAC Gazettes 100,000 Delinquent Companies for Strike-Off Under CAMA Section 692

Source: Punch Nigeria · Corporate Affairs Commission Leadership Assembly in Abuja.

ABUJA — The Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) has published a gazetted list of over 100,000 corporate entities slated for mandatory delisting and strike-off under Section 692 of CAMA 2020. As documented in Punch Nigeria’s coverage of national corporate registry proceedings (featured photo above), the Commission has accelerated enforcement against entities failing to file statutory annual returns and maintain updated registers.

"A company that fails to file statutory annual returns is legally presumed to have ceased operations. Delinquent companies are flagged on our public portal, which directly integrates with commercial banking switches and public procurement portals."
► WHAT THIS MEANS FOR DIRECTORS & OPERATORS:

Being marked "INACTIVE" on the CAC portal is an operational emergency. Commercial banks actively query this registry to enforce Post-No-Debit restrictions, while federal procurement boards automatically disqualify inactive bidders. Settling outstanding annual returns, synchronizing your Persons with Significant Control (PSC) register, and securing an active Status Report immediately restores commercial continuity.
