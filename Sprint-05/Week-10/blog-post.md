## The Scaling Paradox: Why Multi-Campus School Groups Fracture

The typical growth trajectory of an expanding private school group follows a predictable and painful cycle of operational friction:


When schools expand without an integrated institutional operating system, three systemic breaking points emerge:

### 1. Curriculum and Grading Standardization Drift
When a school opens a branch in a new city, it hires local teaching faculty. Without centralized assessment algorithms, teacher grading rubrics diverge. A student scoring 72% in Year 8 English at the flagship campus demonstrates mastery, whereas a student scoring 72% at Branch C has received inflated marks due to lenient local marking. When students sit external standardized exams (Cambridge Checkpoint, IGCSE, or WAEC), the truth emerges—triggering parental disillusionment and brand erosion.

### 2. The Administrative Headcount Spiral
To maintain oversight across distant campuses, leadership instinctively hires more administrative coordinators: campus quality supervisors, bursary auditors, parent liaison officers, and data clerks. Administrative overhead swells, consuming 28% to 35% of total tuition revenue. Yet despite the ballooning payroll, executive management remains chronically under-informed.

### 3. Treasury and Working Capital Fragmentation
Each new campus opens separate local operational accounts with commercial banks. Tuition reconciliations take weeks, cash visibility is fragmented, and uncollected tuition arrears accumulate into tens of millions of Naira. Capital improvement projects at new campuses stall because the group cannot monitor cash flow velocity in real time.
