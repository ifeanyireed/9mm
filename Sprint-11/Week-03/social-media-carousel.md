```text
On a busy Friday afternoon in Lekki, three affluent clients were ready to close multi-million-Naira property and luxury SUV deals on the spot.

Sales reps were celebrating. Receipts were ready to print.

Then the Chief AML Compliance Officer halted every single transaction in sixty seconds—and saved the company from EFCC Post-No-Debit bank freezes.

Here were the three criminal traps hiding inside those "urgent" Friday sales:
• A ₦20M physical cash deposit offered to lock down an off-plan penthouse. Under Section 2 of MLA 2022, accepting cash above ₦5M (individual) or ₦10M (corporate) outside a bank is a strict liability criminal offense.
• A ₦35M luxury SUV cash bundle from a buyer claiming his daily bank transfer limit was maxed out.
• Four separate ₦4.5M transfers sent by four different relatives across three days to avoid single-transaction reporting—classic "smurfing" / structuring under Section 13 of MLA 2022.

With Nigeria's real estate sector contributing 13% to GDP, commercial bank AML algorithms, SCUML, and the NFIU monitor DNFBPs closely. One tainted deposit commingled into operating accounts can trigger Section 18 interim asset forfeiture and company-wide PND freezes.

Protected Nigerian real estate developers and auto merchants deploy 5 non-negotiable guardrails:
1. Enforce a board-approved Zero-Physical-Cash policy across every showroom.
2. Automatically aggregate rolling 7–30 day receipts to flag ₦4.5M structuring ("smurfing") tranches.
3. Require strict Contract-to-Sender name matching—banning unverified third-party deposits.
4. Gate offer letters behind BVN, NIN, CAC UBO, and real-time PEP/sanctions screening.
5. Automate 7-day SCUML CTR filings and confidential 24-hour NFIU STR escalations (zero tipping off).

One illegal cash transaction can freeze everything you built over a decade. Automated AML governance lets you close bigger deals safely.
```
