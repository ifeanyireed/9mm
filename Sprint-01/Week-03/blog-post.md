## The Viral Collection Drop That Turned Into a Stockroom Nightmare

On a Friday evening in Lekki, Chioma's ready-to-wear fashion studio is buzzing with excitement.

She has just posted a reel showcasing her new six-piece collection, available in three colours and four sizes. Within an hour, hundreds of shoppers are commenting and sending messages across Instagram and WhatsApp.

From the outside, the launch looks like a massive success.

But Chioma has a problem.

A customer sends an Instagram message asking if the emerald green two-piece set is still available in Size 12. Because the stock list is written in a paper notebook, Chioma's assistant has to pause replying to messages and walk into the back room to count hangers.

By the time she replies twenty-five minutes later, the customer has gone offline without buying.

Ten minutes after that, a shopper on WhatsApp pays for the last champagne gold dress in Size 10. Almost at the same moment, another shopper on Instagram transfers money for that exact same dress before anyone can cross it out in the notebook.

Now Chioma has two customers expecting one dress, and she has to spend her evening making an uncomfortable apology call and processing a manual refund.

Meanwhile, the dispatch rider returns three parcels from earlier in the week because customers received Size 14 instead of the Size 10 they requested in chat.

Chioma begins to realise something important.

Her business has beautiful designs, eager customers and strong social media demand. What it lacks is a connected system.

Her marketing is moving fast. Her inventory tracking is holding her back.

This is the challenge facing many growing fashion, footwear and beauty brands in Nigeria: offering more sizes, colours and styles attracts more buyers, but managing those choices manually in chat messages creates costly mistakes.

## The Real Problem: Your Social Channels and Stockroom Are Working Separately Instead of Together

Consider Chioma's boutique again.

The packing team prepares parcels using memory and scattered chat screenshots instead of a clear order list.

Everyone on Chioma's team is working hard, yet the business is losing money because its sales conversations, stock counts and delivery slips live in separate places.

Connecting your product variants means putting your sizes, colours, stock numbers and checkout on one shared system.

## 10 Ways to Connect Your Product Variants to Instant Online Sales

### 1. Replace Single-Item Posts with an Organised Multi-Variant Catalogue

When many social sellers upload products, they treat a dress as a single item and rely on chat messages to sort out the size and colour later.

That creates endless back-and-forth questions for both the customer and the sales assistant.

As shown in the retail operations workspace above, setting up a proper multi-variant catalogue (`/erp/admin/inventory`) lets you group all sizes and colours neatly under one main product. Each combination—such as *Emerald Green, Size 12*—gets its own clear stock count and item code.

**Why it matters:** You always know the exact quantity available for every single size and colour without walking to the stockroom.

### 2. Give Customers Interactive Size and Colour Buttons on Your Storefront

Imagine a customer sees Chioma's new dress on Instagram at 10:00 PM.

If she has to send a message asking *"Do you have Medium in blue?"* and wait until morning for a reply, she will likely lose interest.

By directing shoppers to a clean online storefront (`client_slug.ofia.shop/shopfront`) with clickable colour and size buttons, customers can check availability for themselves in seconds. When a shopper taps "Emerald Green", the page immediately shows which sizes are currently in stock.

**Why it matters:** Customers can choose their exact option and buy immediately, even when your team is asleep.

### 3. Lock Stock Automatically While a Customer Is Checking Out

During a busy collection launch, two shoppers often try to buy the last piece of a popular size at the exact same time.

In a manual chat store, both shoppers receive bank account details, both transfer money, and the business is forced to refund one of them.

A connected checkout system prevents this by temporarily reserving the item for a few minutes the moment the first shopper taps through to pay. If the payment finishes, the item is marked sold; if the shopper leaves without paying, the item returns to the store automatically.

**Why it matters:** You stop accidentally selling the same item twice during busy sales spikes.

### 4. Set Up Low-Stock Alerts for Your Fastest-Moving Sizes

In almost every fashion business, certain sizes—like Medium and Large—sell out much faster than others.

When you track inventory in a notebook, you usually discover that a popular size is finished only after three more customers have already asked for it.

An integrated inventory system lets you set simple low-stock alerts for individual sizes. When *Size 12* drops down to its last three pieces, the system notifies your team so you can restock or adjust your promotions early.

**Why it matters:** Your production and buying team can restock popular sizes before you start turning customers away.

### 5. Replace Chat Screenshots with Clear Digital Packing Slips

Think about how orders are packed after a busy weekend sale.

When customers negotiate sizes in WhatsApp chats and send bank transfer screenshots that only show the amount paid, the person packing the parcel has to scroll through old messages to figure out what to put in the bag.

When orders come through a connected storefront, every payment automatically creates a clear digital packing slip (`/erp/admin/logistics/dispatch`) showing the customer's name, address, product photo, exact colour and size code.

**Why it matters:** Your dispatch team packs the right item the first time, cutting down on returns and exchange delivery costs.

### 6. Turn Sold-Out Sizes into Automatic Restock Waitlists

Nothing is more frustrating for a shopper than falling in love with an outfit on Instagram only to hear that her size just sold out.

In most chat-based stores, that conversation simply ends and the customer is lost for good.

With a connected storefront, a sold-out size button can display a simple *"Notify Me When Available"* option where the shopper leaves her phone number or WhatsApp contact. The moment you add new stock for that size, the system can alert everyone who was waiting for it.

**Why it matters:** You capture interested buyers even when a size is temporarily sold out and turn them into easy sales when you restock.

### 7. Use Consistent Colour and Style Names Across Every Channel

Confusion often starts inside the business itself when different staff members call the same item by different names.

One sales assistant might call a dress "Rust Orange" on Instagram, another calls it "Burnt Orange" on WhatsApp, and the stockroom label says "Terracotta".

Creating a single, consistent naming structure inside your inventory system ensures that the exact same colour name and item code appear on your social posts, your online checkout and your stockroom shelves.

**Why it matters:** Clear, consistent naming removes guesswork for both your customers and your staff.

### 8. Connect Inventory Across Your Studio, Showroom and Warehouse

Imagine Chioma keeps half of her stock at her Lekki showroom and the other half at a studio in Ikeja.

A walk-in customer buys the last Size 10 dress in Lekki, while an online assistant is still promising that same dress to a customer on Instagram.

By managing all locations on one multi-location inventory system (`/erp/admin/inventory/warehouses`), any sale at the physical showroom updates your online availability immediately, and orders can be routed to whichever branch has the right size ready to ship.

**Why it matters:** All your locations work from one accurate stock list instead of competing with each other.

### 9. Set Accurate Prices and Delivery Weights for Different Product Options

Not every option in your shop costs the same to produce or deliver.

A three-piece deluxe bundle, a custom length adjustment or a heavier fabric option may have a different price or shipping weight than the standard piece.

Instead of asking sales assistants to calculate extra charges manually in chat, a connected variant system updates the price and delivery fee automatically as soon as the customer selects that specific option on the product page.

**Why it matters:** Customers see transparent, accurate pricing upfront without waiting for a staff member to calculate a quote.

### 10. Review Sales by Size and Colour Before Planning Your Next Production Run

At the end of the month, many boutique owners know how much money they made overall, but they cannot easily see which specific sizes and colours drove those sales—and which ones are sitting untouched on the shelves.

That often leads to producing too many slow-moving sizes in the next collection.

When every order is recorded by variant in your system, you can quickly see which colours and sizes sell out fastest and which ones tie up your cash.

**Why it matters:** You invest your working capital into making more of the exact sizes and colours your customers actually buy.
