**Dear {{first_name|Valued Partner}},**

On a Monday evening in Yaba, a sold-out twelve-week online data analytics and software cohort kicks off with three hundred paying learners from Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and Ibadan typing excited introductions into the welcome chat. From the outside, launch night looks like a triumph.

Yet by Thursday evening, just as Chidi discovered in Cohort 8 of his academy, nearly ninety beginners are stuck fighting local database and software installation errors on older laptops. By Week 3, survivors wait five days for part-time mentors to grade files manually—and past graduates report that hiring managers ignore plain PDF certificates with no online proof of the projects they built.

Yet as cohorts diversify in geography and hardware, traditional academy operations are hitting a retention wall. High dropout rates are rarely caused by lazy learners. They are caused by disconnected video links, chat groups, and manual grading sheets that turn an ambitious student's first week into an exhausting technical obstacle course.
