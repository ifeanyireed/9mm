To a casual user, it looks like standard legal boilerplate.
To an examiner from the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) or an accredited DPCO auditor, it is a smoking gun of non-compliance.

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA 2023), operating with copy-pasted foreign legal policies exposes your startup to administrative fines of up to ₦10,000,000 or 2% of annual gross turnover.

Here are the 3 fatal drafting mistakes that trigger NDPC enforcement inquiries:

1. The "Silicon Valley Jurisdiction" Trap
Foreign templates inevitably declare that all disputes are governed by the laws of California, Delaware, or England & Wales. In Nigeria, such clauses are void against public policy when applied to consumer contracts. Worse, under Sections 41–43 of the NDPA 2023, they signal to regulators that your platform is unlawfully transferring Nigerian citizens' telemetry abroad without verified statutory safeguards.

2. Unlawful Bundled Consent (Section 26 Violation)
Standard US templates use catch-all sentences like: "By using our app, you agree to our terms and the sharing of your data with advertising partners." Under Section 26 of the NDPA 2023, consent must be granular, specific, and freely given. Core service functionality (Contractual Necessity) cannot be held hostage to promotional marketing or third-party advertising SDKs. Single blanket checkboxes make your consent legally void.

3. The Missing Nigerian Redress & DPO Disclosure
Sections 27, 32, and 48 require digital platforms to publish the direct verified email of a designated Nigerian Data Protection Officer (DPO), establish transparent Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) erasure protocols, and inform users of their statutory right to lodge complaints directly with the NDPC.

### Slide 3: Mistake #1 — The Foreign Jurisdiction Trap
* **Headline**: Mistake 1: Designating Foreign Governing Law
* **The Error**: Retaining clauses that name California, Delaware, or England & Wales courts for dispute resolution.
* **Why It Fails**: Void under Nigerian consumer protection law; alerts the NDPC that customer data is being exported without Section 41–43 cross-border adequacy safeguards.
* **The Fix**: Explicitly anchor terms to the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria and the NDPA 2023.

### Slide 4: Mistake #2 — Unlawful Bundled Consent
* **Headline**: Mistake 2: Single "Take-It-Or-Leave-It" Checkboxes
* **The Error**: Forcing users to accept third-party marketing, phonebook scraping, and advertising cookies to create an account.
* **Statutory Violation**: Section 26(3) NDPA 2023 prohibits making core services conditional on non-essential data processing.
* **The Fix**: Unbundle terms: core services rest on Contractual Necessity (§25); marketing telemetry requires separate opt-in toggles.

### Slide 5: Mistake #3 — The Missing Local Redress & DPO
* **Headline**: Mistake 3: Omitting Statutory Nigerian Disclosures
* **Mandatory Disclosures Under NDPA 2023**:
  1. Direct contact details of a named Nigerian Data Protection Officer (DPO).
  2. Clear, programmatic mechanism for Data Subject Access Requests (DSARs) and data erasure.
  3. Explicit notification of the right to lodge complaints with the NDPC under Section 48.
  4. Mandatory 72-hour breach reporting commitment under Section 40.
