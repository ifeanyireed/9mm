3 Expensive Mistakes Nigerian Companies Make When Increasing Share Capital

Dear {{first_name|Valued Partner}},

When a growing Nigerian enterprise prepares for its next growth chapter—whether closing an institutional equity investment, expanding credit lines, or qualifying for public procurement tenders—restructuring equity is unavoidable.

Yet, share capital filings remain the single biggest source of examiner queries on the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) portal. Conflating an increase in issued share capital (allotment) with a secondary share transfer, miscalculating FIRS stamp duties, and missing the mandatory 30-day Persons with Significant Control (PSC) filing under Section 119 of CAMA 2020 routinely freeze corporate bank accounts.

► WHAT THIS MEANS FOR MANAGING DIRECTORS & CORPORATE COUNSEL:
The era of manual discretion and paper waivers at the CAC is over. If your company increases share capital, filings must strictly match FIRS e-Stamp calculations and Section 119 beneficial ownership registers. Any discrepancy triggers immediate portal queries, stalls banking facility updates, and initiates daily late default penalties after 30 calendar days.

The 3 Fatal Share Restructuring Traps

1 Conflating Share Allotment (CAC 2A) with Share Transfer (CAC 2B)

An allotment creates and issues new shares from unissued capital, expanding registered capital and directing funds into the company. A share transfer sells existing shares between shareholders without altering company equity. Conflating the two leaves company balance sheets under-capitalized and causes banks to reject credit drawdowns.
2 Paying FIRS Stamp Duty on Investment Premium Instead of Nominal Capital

Under Section 8 of the Stamp Duties Act, 0.75% ad valorem stamp duty applies strictly to the nominal increase in capital. Inexperienced secretaries frequently assess duty on the entire investment valuation (e.g. paying ₦375,000 on a ₦50M round where nominal capital was only ₦100,000), locking up cash in non-refundable overpayments and causing immediate CAC queries.
3 Overlooking the 30-Day Persons with Significant Control (PSC) Mandate

Under Section 119 of CAMA 2020, any allotment granting an investor 5% or more equity requires concurrent PSC beneficial ownership disclosure. Missing the 30-day window activates non-negotiable daily compounding default fines against both the company and every individual director.
