### Long-Form Thought Leadership Post
```text
In wholesale distribution, revenue is not recognized when goods leave your warehouse.
It is recognized when the buyer’s finance department verifies delivery.

If your distribution business moves ₦300M a month, but your drivers take 18 days to bring back signed paper waybills from regional depots, consider your operational reality:

Floating ₦180M in unbilled inventory across interstate corridors for 18 days ties up valuable working capital and triggers millions in monthly commercial debt financing.

Why does this happen? Because traditional paper-based wholesale invoicing suffers from three chronic friction points:
• Grease-stained, illegible carbon slips that accounts payable teams reject
• Disputed carton damages with zero photographic proof or audit trails
• Drivers on long-haul routes carrying the only physical copy of the delivery note for weeks

When a depot receiving clerk crosses out 5 damaged cartons on a handwritten waybill with a ballpoint pen, corporate buyers routinely withhold payment on the ENTIRE 500-carton shipment until a credit note is physically reconciled.

Top-tier distributors don't wait for trucks to return.
They link warehouse bin picking directly to mobile electronic Proof of Delivery (ePOD):

1. Barcode scanning at loading docks deducts warehouse bin inventory in real time.
2. Drivers capture digital signatures and high-resolution photo damage proof on glass at customer receiving bays.
3. Automated split-invoicing generates an immediate credit note for damaged items and dispatches a verified, clean electronic invoice for accepted stock in 4 seconds.
```
