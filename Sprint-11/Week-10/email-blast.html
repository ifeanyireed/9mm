The 3 Expensive DNFBP Quarterly Filing Blunders

1 Submitting Returns with Defective Customer Identification (The "Phantom Buyer" Trap)

Under Section 4 and Section 10 of MLA 2022, every DNFBP must verify customer identities using National Identity Numbers (NIN), BVNs, and proof of address before concluding sales. Submitting returns with nicknames (e.g. "Chief Alhaji") or unverified third-party corporate entities triggers immediate flags on SCUML's database, resulting in ₦5,000,000 administrative penalties.
2 The "Split Payment" Illusion (Structuring to Evade Cash Ceilings)

Section 2 of MLA 2022 strictly prohibits cash payments above ₦5,000,000 for individuals and ₦10,000,000 for corporates. Splitting an ₦18,000,000 transaction into multiple sub-threshold cash installments across consecutive days to avoid a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) is classified as criminal structuring ("smurfing") under Section 18, attracting mandatory fines of ₦10,000,000 or 100% of the structured amount.
3 Missing Annual Staff Training Logs and Internal Compliance Manuals

Under Section 11 of MLA 2022, every DNFBP must maintain a documented AML policy, appoint a compliance officer, and retain verifiable evidence of annual staff AML training workshops. Over 75% of inspected merchants have no documented staff training registers, leading examiners to invalidate their compliance standing on-site.
