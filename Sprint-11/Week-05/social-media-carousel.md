Under Nigeria’s United Nations-backed goAML architecture, commercial banks don't ask your permission before reporting high-value inflows. The moment an electronic wire transfer exceeding ₦5,000,000 (for individuals) or ₦10,000,000 (for corporate entities) settles, core banking software automatically pushes the transaction metadata into the NFIU goAML platform.

Most high-value commercial operators don't realize they have triggered an AML investigation until their bank accounts freeze.

Here are the 3 unmistakable warning signs that your business is in the regulatory crosshairs:

1. The Symmetrical 7-Day CTR Gap: Under Section 11 of the Money Laundering Act 2022, reporting is a two-way handshake. When your bank reports the inflow, the NFIU expects a matching Currency Transaction Report (CTR) from your company within 7 days. If you don't file, an automated flag fires.
2. Unscreened PEP Inflows: Accepting high-value payments from public office holders or senior civil servants without documented Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) escalates your corporate file directly to the EFCC Special Fraud Unit.
3. Split Payment Structuring ("Smurfing"): Accommodating buyers who transfer funds in multiple sub-₦5M tranches to evade limits is flagged by bank AI under Section 13, triggering automated Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs).

When these triggers fire, commercial banks enforce automated Post-No-Debit (PND) orders to shield themselves from CBN penalties, bringing operations to a sudden standstill.
