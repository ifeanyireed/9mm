### Editor's Opening Letter
**Dear {{first_name|Valued Partner}},**

In fast-paced business operations, decisions are made in hours: a co-founder leaves, a new strategic investor comes on board, or the company moves to a larger headquarters.

Yet many executive boards make a costly assumption: believing that signing a board resolution internally completes the process.

Under Nigerian law, internal resolutions are mere intentions until they are registered with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). When unfiled board changes clash with real-time commercial bank KYC checks, the consequences are immediate: rejected signatures, frozen corporate accounts, and stalled investment rounds.

In this edition, we examine the legal mechanics of post-incorporation regularizations, the risks of unrecorded share allotments, and how to keep your corporate filings in 100% harmony with reality.

### Cover Story: Corporate Governance & Statutory Enforcement
**HEADLINE: [CAC Mandates Strict 14-Day Timeline for Post-Incorporation Disclosures](https://punchng.com/cac-mandates-strict-timelines-for-post-incorporation-filings/)**

* <img src="https://wsrv.nl/?url=https://cdn.punchng.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/24150114/WhatsApp-Image-2026-09-24-at-3.00.43-PM_1790258474.webp&w=800&output=jpg" alt="Corporate Affairs Commission Regulatory Summit on CAMA 2020 Compliance" referrerpolicy="no-referrer" width="100%" />
* **Source**: Punch Nigeria · [Read Full Story →](https://punchng.com/cac-mandates-strict-timelines-for-post-incorporation-filings/)

**ABUJA** — The Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) has issued an operational circular reminding incorporated entities of the statutory timelines governing post-incorporation changes under CAMA 2020. As documented in Punch Nigeria's coverage of corporate governance proceedings (featured photo above), the commission is stepping up automated compliance cross-checks between post-incorporation filings, annual returns, and beneficial ownership registries.

The Registrar-General noted that neglecting post-incorporation updates exposes companies to compounding administrative penalties and commercial disruption:

> "A company's official record on the public registry must reflect reality. Alterations to directorship, share allotments, or registered office addresses that are not registered on the portal within statutory deadlines are legally ineffective and attract compounding daily fines."

👉 **[Read full story at Punch Nigeria →](https://punchng.com/cac-mandates-strict-timelines-for-post-incorporation-filings/)**

> **► WHAT THIS MEANS FOR MANAGING DIRECTORS & COMPANY SECRETARIES:**
> Operating with outdated board records creates severe banking vulnerabilities. By regularizing director appointments, updating share allotment schedules, and synchronizing Section 119 Persons with Significant Control (PSC) registers, your business avoids bank mandate rejections and ensures uninterrupted commercial banking transactions.
