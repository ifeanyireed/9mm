3 Fatal Mistakes Contractors Make in the Technical Bid Envelope

Dear {{first_name|Valued Partner}},

In Nigerian public procurement, winning a federal contract is often treated as an engineering or commercial pricing challenge. Contractors believe that if their engineering design is sound and their pricing is competitive, the contract award is assured.

Under the Public Procurement Act 2007, this is a fatal misconception. Over 65% of all bids submitted to federal ministries, departments, and agencies (MDAs) are disqualified during preliminary examination—before the financial envelope is ever cut open. A single unsworn affidavit, an uncertified payment slip in place of a PENCOM certificate, or a turnover contradiction between your TCC and your audited accounts triggers immediate, non-negotiable disqualification.

Bureau of Public Procurement Reaffirms Zero-Tolerance for Defective Preliminary Bidding Documents

The Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP) has issued a nationwide regulatory directive to all federal Ministerial Tenders Boards (MTBs). The circular mandates evaluators to strictly enforce preliminary responsiveness criteria under Section 16(6) of the Public Procurement Act 2007. Bidders submitting un-notarized affidavits, pending statutory application receipts, or mismatched audited turnover records must be disqualified immediately during the public opening session without administrative waivers.

► WHAT THIS MEANS FOR CONTRACTORS & BID MANAGERS:
Evaluators are legally prohibited from granting discretion. If your technical envelope contains an administrative defect, your bid will be rejected in public before civil society observers and competing bidders. Your commercial pricing envelope will remain unopened, regardless of how competitive your figures are. Preliminary compliance must be 100% flawless before you seal the envelope.

The 3 Fatal Technical Envelope Pitfalls

1 The Unsworn or Incomplete BPP Affidavit

Under Section 16(6) of PPA 2007, bidders must provide an affidavit addressing five mandatory limbs, including declaring that no BPP or procuring entity staff has any pecuniary interest in the firm. Having a private lawyer merely stamp the document with an NBA seal is an automatic disqualification—it MUST be sworn before a Commissioner for Oaths in a High Court or a certified Notary Public.
2 The "Application Slip" Trap (PENCOM, ITF & NSITF)

Under BPP Circular BPP/R&M/08/Vol.I/01, evaluators are strictly forbidden from accepting Remita payment receipts, application letters, or pending acknowledgement slips in lieu of certified statutory clearance certificates. Preliminary evaluation is binary: without the final certified certificate, the bid is disqualified.
3 The Three-Way Financial Contradiction (TCC vs Audits vs BPP Portal)

Evaluators cross-reference turnover declared on your FIRS Tax Clearance Certificate against figures in your Audited Financials and your BPP National Database Profile (IRR). Any unexplained numerical discrepancy (e.g. ₦380M on TCC vs ₦450M in Audit) constitutes conflicting statutory evidence and results in immediate elimination.
