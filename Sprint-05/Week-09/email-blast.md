### The Anatomy of the ₦54M Tuition Bleed

Private schools operate on rigid, unforgiving fixed costs. Faculty payroll must clear on the 25th of the month, diesel generators consume millions in fuel weekly, and facility maintenance cannot wait.

When tuition collection stalls, the entire educational enterprise suffers.

When our audit team at **ResultsPro** analyzed Crown Heights International Schools (1,480 students across Lekki, Ikeja, and Abuja), we quantified their **Uncollected Tuition Drag (UTD)**:

$$\text{UTD} = \sum_{c=1}^{K} \left[ \left(\text{DF}_c \times \text{WACC} \times \frac{\text{DP}_c}{365}\right) + \text{FR}_c + (\text{NPS}_c \times \text{HRS} \times \text{BWR}) + (\text{GSC}_c \times \text{LTV}_{\text{stu}})\right]$$

The audited annual losses were eye-opening:
* **₦15.0M in Overdraft Carrying Costs**: Borrowing at 28% APR to bridge cash deficits while ₦145M in tuition sat uncollected past mid-term.
* **₦8.4M in Fraudulent Receipts & Bad Debts**: Photoshopped mobile transfer PDFs and forged tellers stamped by overworked staff.
* **₦9.6M in Manual Reconciliation Labor**: 8 bursary staff spending 160 hours per term cross-referencing bank statements against student ledgers.
* **₦21.0M in Gate Humiliation Attrition**: 5 affluent students withdrawn after gate security mistakenly turned them away due to out-of-sync payment records.

Total annual financial bleed: **₦54,028,767 every single year**.
