## The Fast-Growing Brand That Was Working for Its Marketplace Platform

On a Friday afternoon, Zainab's skincare studio in Surulere, Lagos, is packed with outbound parcels—facing the exact margin squeeze reflected in Punch Nigeria's commercial finance coverage above.

Her team bubble-wraps amber bottles of vitamin C serum and seals jars of whipped shea butter for hundreds of third-party marketplace orders. From the outside, Zainab's brand looks like a breakout hit.

But Zainab has a problem.

At month-end, her settlement statement exposes the math: on every ₦10,000 skincare set, ingredients and packaging leave a 22% gross margin. Then the marketplace slices 15% off the gross price—plus sponsored search fees just to stay on page one—wiping out nearly three-quarters of her actual operating profit.

Next morning, a courier returns twelve pay-on-delivery boxes rejected after five-day delivery delays, and the platform deducts return logistics penalties on every scuffed parcel. Meanwhile, her bottle supplier wants cash upfront while her revenue sits frozen inside a 14-day holding cycle, and masked customer phone numbers prevent her from messaging repeat buyers directly.

Her business has great products, loyal fans and strong order volume. What it lacks is an independent storefront she actually controls.
