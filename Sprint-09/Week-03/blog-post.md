## 1. The Relatable Opening Persona Story: The Ikoyi Boardroom Frozen by a Phantom Public Register

On a bright Wednesday afternoon in Ikoyi, Lagos, Chief Raymond Okafor sits across the polished conference table from his Company Secretary, Barrister Amina Bello, preparing to close a ₦450 million working-capital and equipment expansion facility for *Okafor Meridian Holdings Ltd*. Over the past three years, the corporate group has grown rapidly across industrial trading, commercial property, and supply chain logistics.

Inside the boardroom, every milestone felt decisively executed. As commercial banking partnerships across Nigeria expand credit to growing enterprises—reflected in Punch Nigeria's coverage of commercial bank SME support above—Chief Raymond assumed his group's strong balance sheet and blue-chip reputation guaranteed smooth execution.

*But Chief Raymond has a problem.*

Just forty-eight hours before the bank facility drawdown and vendor settlement window, an urgent automated compliance notice lands from his Tier-1 commercial bank: corporate account debit mandates have been suspended pending resolution of a **"DIRECTOR BVN MISMATCH & UNRECORDED BOARD STRUCTURE"** flag. Pulling a live CAC Electronic Status Report on the Company Registration Portal (CRP), Chief Raymond and Barrister Amina uncover three devastating post-incorporation blindspots:

### 1. A Resigned Founding Director Still Listed on the Official CAC Registry
Two years earlier, a co-founding director retired amicably and relocated abroad, while Chief Raymond's operations chief was promoted to Executive Director via signed board minutes. Because nobody filed **Form CAC 7A** within the statutory 14-day window under Section 321 of CAMA 2020, the bank's automated CAC API lookup rejected the new Executive Director's BVN as an unauthorized stranger—and froze outbound mandates under anti-money laundering (AML) Know-Your-Customer rules.

### 2. Unfiled Share Allotments Blocking Equity Due Diligence and Section 119 PSC Updates
Eighteen months ago, a private investment syndicate injected ₦120 million into *Okafor Meridian Holdings* in exchange for 20% equity. Handshakes were exchanged and internal shareholder agreements signed, yet **Form CAC 2A (Return of Allotment)** and Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) stamp duty assessments were never filed on the CRP. Worse, the company's **Section 119 Persons with Significant Control (PSC)** register contradicted the new shareholding—causing the CAC portal to reject routine annual returns outright.

### 3. An Outdated Registered Office Address Jeopardizing Statutory Notices
When the group moved its headquarters from Yaba to Ikoyi twenty months ago, the admin desk updated letterheads and website footers—but never filed **Form CAC 3** within 14 days as required under Section 722 of CAMA 2020. Two critical FIRS tax assessments and a regulatory correspondence letter had been dispatched to the vacant Yaba building, exposing the group to avoidable default penalties.

Chief Raymond sets down the bank restriction letter and looks across the Ikoyi skyline. His corporate group has audited revenues, real assets, and committed institutional partners. What it lacks is a synchronized post-incorporation governance engine.

### 2. Regularize Director Appointments and Resignations Immediately (Form CAC 7A)

When a director resigns, retires, or joins the board without formal registration, the retired director remains legally accountable for company liabilities while the newly appointed executive cannot pass commercial bank mandate verification.

Obtain a signed resignation letter from every departed director, draft an authoritative Board Resolution formally accepting the resignation or approving the appointment, and file **Form CAC 7A** on the CRP immediately—and strictly within **14 days** for every future change under Section 321 of CAMA 2020.

**Why it matters:** Aligns your legal boardroom authority with the public registry so bank signatory updates clear smoothly without AML holds.

---

### 3. File Return of Share Allotments and Clear FIRS Stamp Duty (Form CAC 2A)

Handing new equity investors signed internal shareholder agreements or printed share certificates without formal CAC filing leaves their ownership legally unperfected—creating instant deal-breakers during subsequent institutional funding rounds.

Assess and settle statutory **FIRS stamp duty** on all share capital increases or new equity issuances first, then file **Form CAC 2A (Return of Allotment)** on the Company Registration Portal within one month so every shareholder's exact unit count appears cleanly on the Certified Status Report.

**Why it matters:** Transforms informal equity promises into an auditable, investor-ready cap table recognized by courts, lenders, and private equity funds.

---

### 4. Harmonize the Section 119 Persons with Significant Control (PSC) Register

Under Section 119 of CAMA 2020, every individual or corporate body holding **5% or more equity, voting rights, or control** must be recorded on the national PSC database. The CAC portal now programmatically blocks annual returns whenever PSC disclosures contradict recent share allotments or transfers.

Update your Section 119 PSC ledger concurrently with every Form CAC 2A share allotment or share transfer deed—documenting exact ownership percentages, dates of acquisition, and verified identity details cleanly on the CRP.

**Why it matters:** Eliminates hard CRP portal lockouts during urgent filings and satisfies strict global beneficial ownership transparency rules.
