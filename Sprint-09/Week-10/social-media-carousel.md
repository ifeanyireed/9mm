## 1. LinkedIn (Corporate Governance & Institutional Strategy)

### Long-Form Thought Leadership Post
```text
A growing Nigerian logistics company raised ₦50M from an angel syndicate last month.

The founders agreed to allocate 10% equity to the new investors.
Then their company secretary made a ₦374,000 blunder that delayed their bank facility for 6 weeks.

Here is what went wrong (and why Nigerian founders must understand the difference between Share Allotment and Share Transfer):

Mistake 1: Confusing an Allotment with a Transfer
When you raise fresh capital for your business, you must execute a SHARE ALLOTMENT (Form CAC 2A). You are creating brand-new shares from your company's unissued capital pool. The ₦50M investment goes into the company's bank account for business operations.
If you file a SHARE TRANSFER (Form CAC 2B), you are selling existing shares owned by a founder. The ₦50M legally belongs to the selling founder personally—the company balance sheet gets ZERO equity capital.

Mistake 2: The FIRS Stamp Duty Overpayment
Under the Stamp Duties Act, ad valorem stamp duty on share capital increases is 0.75%.
BUT 0.75% OF WHAT?
The secretary entered the full ₦50M valuation into the FIRS portal.
FIRS assessed 0.75% of ₦50M = ₦375,000. The company paid it on Remita.
In reality, to give 10% equity, the company only created 100,000 ordinary shares at ₦1.00 nominal value each, issued at a premium of ₦499 per share!
The correct stamp duty was 0.75% on the NOMINAL increase (100,000 shares × ₦1.00 = ₦100,000).
₦100,000 × 0.75% = ₦750!
The remaining ₦49.9M was Share Premium under Section 145 of CAMA 2020—which is completely exempt from ad valorem stamp duty!
The company overpaid FIRS by ₦374,250.
And worse: CAC examiners rejected the filing because the EGM board resolution (₦100k nominal increase) did not reconcile with the FIRS stamp certificate (₦50M)!

Mistake 3: Missing the 30-Day PSC Filing Cliff
Under Section 119 of CAMA 2020, any allotment giving an investor 5% or more equity requires a mandatory Persons with Significant Control (PSC) filing within 30 days.
If you submit Form CAC 2A without the PSC beneficial ownership notice, CRP 2.0 flags an immediate query. Miss the 30-day window, and daily compounding penalties hit both the company and every individual director.
```
