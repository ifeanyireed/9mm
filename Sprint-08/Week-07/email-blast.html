In state government administration, the most destructive conflict isn't between political parties. It is the silent, territorial bureaucratic cold war between statutory agencies.

Look at the governance structure of public education in any state: SUBEB exercises jurisdiction over primary and junior secondary schools, maintaining paper ledgers across local government authorities. The Ministry of Education oversees senior secondary schools, maintaining a separate payroll spreadsheet in the secretariat. The Civil Service Commission holds statutory authority over appointments, confirmations, and promotion gazettes, storing physical records in central archives. And the Office of the State Accountant-General disburses electronic salaries based on unverified bank lists.

Because these statutory agencies operate as disconnected technological archipelagos, public funds bleed into duplicate salary disbursements, delayed promotions, and ghost mandates. You cannot solve inter-agency friction with expensive ad-hoc committees; you need an automated cryptographic consensus rail. Here is your operational briefing.
