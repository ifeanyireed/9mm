### Part 3: Deep Dive — 3 Fatal Mistakes Contractors Make in the Technical Bid Envelope

#### Mistake 1: The Unsworn or Incomplete BPP Affidavit
Under Section 16(6) of PPA 2007, bidders must provide a sworn affidavit addressing five mandatory limbs: non-insolvency, lack of criminal conviction for directors, document authenticity, legal competence, and crucially, declaring that *no current or former staff of the procuring entity or BPP has any pecuniary interest in the bidder*. Contractors routinely fail by having a private lawyer merely stamp the document with an NBA seal. Under Nigerian law, an affidavit is only legally valid if sworn before a **Commissioner for Oaths in a High Court** or a recognized **Notary Public**.

#### Mistake 2: The "Application Slip" Illusion (PENCOM, ITF & NSITF)
When federal procurement adverts drop, contractors often find their annual clearance certificates pending. Desperate to meet the deadline, they attach Remita payment receipts or stamped application letters. Under BPP Circular BPP/R&M/08/Vol.I/01, evaluators are strictly forbidden from accepting application slips. Preliminary evaluation is binary: you either possess a valid, verifiable certificate, or you are marked **NON-RESPONSIVE** and disqualified.

#### Mistake 3: The Three-Way Financial Contradiction (TCC vs. Audits vs. BPP Portal)
Evaluators cross-reference three documents during preliminary screening: your 3-year Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC), your 3-year Audited Accounts, and your BPP National Database Profile (IRR). If your tax accountant minimized turnover on TaxPro-Max while your auditor maximized turnover to prove high capacity, the discrepancy (e.g. ₦380M on TCC vs ₦450M on Audit) constitutes conflicting statutory evidence, resulting in immediate preliminary disqualification.
