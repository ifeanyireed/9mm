Over 100,000 Nigerian companies have been gazetted for involuntary strike-off and dissolution by the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC).

Most managing directors believe that because they have a framed certificate of incorporation on their boardroom wall, their company is legally safe forever.

Inside Nigerian corporate law, incorporation is not a permanent right—it is a conditional license.

Under Section 692 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 (CAMA 2020), failing to file Annual Returns for two consecutive years gives the Registrar-General full legal power to strike your business off the register.

When that happens, the consequences are catastrophic:
• Your company ceases to exist as a legal entity.
• Under Section 693, all corporate assets and bank balances legally vest in the Federal Government of Nigeria as "bona vacantia" (unowned property).
• Directors lose limited liability protection for all ongoing commercial obligations.

The real danger is not just strike-off; it is the operational paralysis that hits months before the gazette is published.
