## 1. The Relatable Opening Persona Story: The Busy Computer Village Merchant Who Was Working for His Landlord

On a lively Wednesday afternoon along Otigba Street in Computer Village, Ikeja, the showroom and fulfillment desk of *ZenoTech Gadgets & Computing* is humming. Sealed boxes of MacBooks, enterprise HP workstations, power stations, and original accessories line reinforced glass shelves. Every few minutes, couriers stop by to collect neatly bubble-wrapped parcels headed for Victoria Island, Lekki, Port Harcourt, and Abuja.

Running this high-velocity electronics business is Chuka, a sharp, detail-oriented merchant who built *ZenoTech* over six years from a single repair-and-accessories counter into one of Computer Village's most trusted specialist dealers. Like thousands of ambitious Nigerian sellers, Chuka originally leaned on third-party aggregators like Jumia and classified boards like Jiji to jumpstart online sales without building custom e-commerce software. As seen in Punch Nigeria's coverage of [Federal Government and Jumia partnerships expanding e-commerce reach](https://punchng.com/fg-jumia-partner-to-expand-e-commerce-digital-inclusion/) (featured photo above), large marketplace platforms undeniably helped introduce millions of Nigerians to online shopping.

*But Chuka has a problem.*

Once *ZenoTech* achieved strong product-market fit and scaled to high monthly volume, the aggregator business model flipped from a launchpad into a margin trap. Chuka is moving more inventory every quarter, yet his net retained profit keeps shrinking and his working capital feels perpetually strangled.

Every single week, Chuka faces three painful commercial micro-failures created by renting shelf space on third-party marketplaces:

### 1. The 15%–20% Commission and Boosted-Ad Margin Crush
At 11:20 AM on Wednesday, *ZenoTech* sells a brand-new ₦950,000 business laptop through a major marketplace aggregator. In Computer Village's fiercely competitive electronics sector, healthy gross margins sit around 18% to 22% before rent, diesel, and staff payroll.

Yet once the marketplace deducts its category commission, mandatory shipping-hub handling fees, and pay-to-play visibility boosts required just to appear on page one—or the monthly "Diamond Boost" fees on Jiji just to outrank unverified impersonators—between 15% and 20% of the transaction value evaporates straight to the platform. Chuka absorbs all the import risk, warranty liability, and warehouse overhead while the aggregator pockets the bulk of his net margin.

### 2. The Fourteen-Day Cash Flow Freeze and Return-Penalty Trap
On Thursday, Chuka needs liquid cash to lock in a fresh batch of discounted laptops from his distributor before exchange-rate shifts push replacement costs higher. He has over ₦14,000,000 in completed, delivered marketplace orders sitting inside his seller dashboard—but the aggregator enforces a rigid ten-to-fourteen-day payout reconciliation hold.

Worse, when a buyer triggers a frivolous return on a high-ticket gadget because they changed their mind after opening the box, the platform automatically debits Chuka's pending payout balance, charges a return logistics penalty, and leaves him holding an unsealed device he can no longer sell as brand new.

### 3. The Masked Customer Number and Competitor Hijacking
At 4:00 PM, an enterprise buyer who purchased three workstations from *ZenoTech* via an aggregator logs back onto the marketplace to buy five more units for his expanding team. Because the aggregator deliberately masks buyer phone numbers and emails behind anonymized relay systems, Chuka never received his own customer's direct contact details.

When that repeat buyer clicks *ZenoTech*'s laptop listing, the marketplace prominently displays a "Cheaper Sellers Also Offering This Item" banner right below Chuka's product photos—handing his hard-won repeat buyer straight to a cheaper, grey-market rival. Chuka has no owned customer list, no direct WhatsApp relationship, and zero lasting brand equity.

His business has authentic inventory, technical credibility, and booming demand. What it lacks is a sovereign, connected system.
