### ACT II: WHITEBOARD TEARDOWN — THE FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS (1:35 – 3:00)

**Visual**: Host at the digital whiteboard. Writing fast, precise numbers.

**HOST**:
When you issue new shares in Nigeria, two government bodies collect fees:
First, the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) collects **ad valorem stamp duty**.
Second, the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) collects **registration filing fees**.

Under Section 8 of the Stamp Duties Act, stamp duty is **0.75%**.
**BUT 0.75% OF WHAT?**

*(Host writes large in RED)*
**WRONG WAY**:
Investor invests ₦50,000,000 for 10% equity.
The inexperienced secretary inputs ₦50,000,000 as the nominal capital on the FIRS e-Stamp portal.
0.75% of ₦50,000,000 = **₦375,000**.
The company pays ₦375,000 via Remita.

Now look at what the company actually created:
*(Host writes in GREEN)*
**THE RIGHT WAY (UNDER CAMA 2020)**:
To give the investor 10% equity, the company created 100,000 ordinary shares at a **nominal value of ₦1.00 each**, issued at a **premium of ₦499.00 per share**!
Total nominal increase: **100,000 shares × ₦1.00 = ₦100,000**.
The remaining ₦49,900,000 is **Share Premium** under Section 145 of CAMA 2020!
And share premium is **completely exempt from ad valorem stamp duty**!

Let's calculate the correct duty:
₦100,000 nominal capital × 0.75% = **₦750.00**!

*(Host circles ₦750.00 and points to ₦375,000)*
They paid ₦375,000 instead of ₦750! That’s an overpayment of **₦374,250**!
And worse: when the CAC examiner compared the board resolution—which said nominal capital increased by ₦100,000—against the FIRS stamp certificate showing ₦50,000,000, the algorithm triggered an immediate query rejection!
