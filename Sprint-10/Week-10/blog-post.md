## The Public Procurement Paradox: Why the Lowest Price Never Gets Read

In Nigerian public procurement, there is a dangerous misconception shared by inexperienced contractors:
> *"If our engineering design is world-class and our pricing is the lowest in the room, we are guaranteed to win the ministry contract."*

This assumption ignores the fundamental two-stage structure mandated by the **Public Procurement Act 2007 (PPA 2007)**:
1. **Stage 1: Preliminary Technical Evaluation (Pass / Fail)**
2. **Stage 2: Detailed Technical & Financial Evaluation (Scored / Commercial)**

Under Section 23 and Section 32 of PPA 2007, tender evaluators are strictly forbidden from opening the **Financial Bid Envelope (Envelope B)** of any contractor whose **Technical Bid Envelope (Envelope A)** fails preliminary responsiveness.

If your technical envelope has a single administrative defect, your bid is disqualified on the spot. Your financial envelope is returned to you unopened, or archived as non-responsive. The months spent refining your bill of engineering measurement and evaluation (BEME) and slashing your margins count for nothing.

As shown in Figure 1 below (Source: Nairametrics), public bid opening sessions in Abuja federal ministries are conducted in public before civil society observers, anti-corruption monitoring groups, and competing bidders. Tenders board evaluators have zero legal discretion to waive statutory defects.

## Mistake #1: The Unsworn or Defective BPP Sworn Affidavit

Under Standard Bidding Documents issued by the Bureau of Public Procurement, every bidder must provide a **Sworn Affidavit** certifying five mandatory statutory declarations under Section 16(6) of PPA 2007:
1. That the company is not in receivership, insolvent, or bankrupt.
2. That none of the company’s directors has ever been convicted in any court for a criminal offence relating to fraud, financial impropriety, or professional misconduct.
3. That no former or current staff of the procuring entity or the Bureau of Public Procurement is a current or former director, shareholder, or has any pecuniary interest in the bidder.
4. That all documents submitted in the bid are genuine, authentic, and verifiable.
5. That the company possesses the legal capacity and technical competence to enter into the procurement contract.

### Where Contractors Fail Catastrophically
* **The "Lawyer's Stamp" Blunder**: Many contractors have their retained corporate lawyer draft the affidavit, stamp it with an NBA seal, and attach it to the tender package. **This is an automatic disqualification.** A statutory affidavit under Nigerian law (Oaths Act, Cap O1, LFN 2004) can only be administered by a **Commissioner for Oaths** in a High Court registry or a recognized **Notary Public**. An ordinary legal practitioner's stamp does not constitute a sworn legal oath.
* **The Defective Language Trap**: The affidavit must address all five statutory limbs. Tenders board evaluators utilize rigid checklists. If an affidavit covers insolvency, non-conviction, and document authenticity, but omits the explicit declaration that *no staff of the procuring entity or BPP has a pecuniary interest in the company*, the bid is marked **NON-RESPONSIVE** and discarded immediately.
* **The Expired Oath**: While affidavits technically remain valid for the facts stated at the time of swearing, federal MDAs generally require affidavits sworn within the **current procurement cycle or calendar year**. Submitting a sworn affidavit from a prior year’s tender package is an instant red flag that triggers disqualification.

## Mistake #2: The "Application Slip" Illusion (PENCOM, ITF & NSITF)

In federal contracting, time is always against the bidder. Ministries publish procurement adverts in national daily newspapers and the Federal Tenders Journal giving contractors a **mandatory 6-week window** to prepare and submit bids.

Contractors suddenly discover that their annual statutory quad clearance certificates are either expired or pending renewal:
* Their PENCOM compliance certificate expired on December 31st.
* Their Industrial Training Fund (ITF) Form 5A has not been issued.
* Their NSITF ECA-01 compliance certificate is trapped in payroll audit reconciliation.

Desperate to meet the 12:00 PM bid submission deadline, the contractor pays the statutory remittances via Remita, gathers the payment receipts and formal application letters, and slips them into the technical envelope alongside an explanatory cover note:
> *"Please note that our PENCOM renewal has been paid and is currently being processed at the National Pension Commission headquarters. Attached is our Remita payment receipt and stamped acknowledgement copy of our renewal application."*

### Why This Guarantees Immediate Disqualification
Under Section 16(6)(d) of PPA 2007 and BPP Circular Ref: BPP/R&M/08/Vol.I/01, tender evaluators are strictly prohibited from accepting **payment receipts, acknowledgement slips, or pending application letters** in place of a certified statutory clearance certificate.

The procurement rule is binary:
* You either possess a valid, verifiable **Compliance Certificate** issued by the statutory agency.
* Or you do **NOT** possess it.

During preliminary envelope opening, the evaluator checks:
$$\text{Item: PENCOM Certificate} \longrightarrow \text{Status: REMITA RECEIPT ATTACHED} \longrightarrow \text{Verdict: NON-RESPONSIVE}$$

The evaluator does not call the National Pension Commission. They do not grant a 7-day grace period. Under procurement law, granting one contractor a waiver while holding others to the letter of the law violates the core principle of **equal opportunity and competitive fairness**. The bid is disqualified immediately.

## Mistake #3: The Three-Way Financial Contradiction (FIRS TCC vs. Audits vs. BPP Portal)

The third fatal error is a mathematical and administrative contradiction between the contractor’s financial submissions across three mandatory documents:
1. **The Three-Year Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC)** issued by the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS).
2. **The Three-Year Audited Financial Statements** signed by an ICAN/ANAN certified chartered accountant.
3. **The Contractor's Categorization and Classification Profile** on the official BPP National Database of Contractors, Consultants, and Service Providers.

### The Reconciliation Triad
Consider what happens when a contractor submits the following figures for Fiscal Year 2025:

| Document | Declared Annual Turnover | Operating Profit / Tax Paid | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Audited Financials** | ₦450,000,000 | ₦45,000,000 Operating Profit | Signed by External Auditor |
| **FIRS TCC** | ₦380,000,000 | ₦11,400,000 CIT Assessed | **₦70M Turnover Discrepancy** |
| **BPP Portal Profile** | ₦200,000,000 | Legacy classification not updated | **₦250M Variance with Audit** |

Why does this happen?
* In many contracting firms, the tax accountant works in a silo to minimize tax liabilities on TaxPro-Max, resulting in an understated turnover figure on the TCC.
* Meanwhile, the tender director uses an audited financial report that maximizes turnover to prove high financial capacity to the procuring ministry.
* And the company's BPP portal profile was registered three years ago and has never been updated with recent audited financial uploads.

### The Immediate Disqualification Trigger
When the technical evaluation committee places the TCC beside the audited accounts, the ₦70,000,000 variance stands out immediately.

Under Section 16(6)(e) and Section 32 of PPA 2007:
* A variance between the TCC and the audited accounts constitutes **false representation or conflicting statutory evidence**.
* Furthermore, if the BPP National Database Interim Registration Report (IRR) reflects an un-renewed classification, the contractor’s **BPP registration is deemed lapsed**.

The committee marks the bid **NON-RESPONSIVE for conflicting financial documentation**. The contractor is disqualified without the evaluation committee ever reviewing their engineering methodology or opening their financial envelope.

## The Zero-Disqualification Pre-Bid Technical Checklist

To guarantee that your federal bids survive preliminary examination and advance to the financial opening, execute this 10-point audit before sealing Envelope A:

### 1. Mandatory Statutory Clearances (The "Statutory Quad")
* [ ] **FIRS Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC)**: Valid for the current year (covering the last 3 preceding years). Turnover and tax paid on TCC must reconcile 100% with audited accounts.
* [ ] **PENCOM Compliance Certificate**: Valid until December 31st of the current fiscal year. Certified for at least 15 staff (or minimum required by tender advert).
* [ ] **ITF Clearance Certificate (Form 5A)**: Valid until December 31st of the current fiscal year, confirming full 1% payroll levy contribution.
* [ ] **NSITF Compliance Certificate (ECA-01)**: Valid until December 31st of the current fiscal year, confirming 1% gross payroll employees' compensation contribution.

### 2. Legal Charter & Procurement Registration
* [ ] **CAC Registration Pack**: Certificate of Incorporation, Form CAC 1.1 / Status Report showing **STATUS: ACTIVE**, and Memorandum & Articles of Association.
* [ ] **BPP Interim Registration Report (IRR)**: Valid and active on the National Database of Contractors, Consultants, and Service Providers, with financial capacity classification matching the tender category.

### 3. Sworn Affidavit & Bid Security
* [ ] **Sworn Affidavit**: Duly sworn before a Commissioner for Oaths in a High Court or a certified Notary Public (NOT merely stamped by a private lawyer). Must address all 5 statutory limbs of Section 16(6) PPA 2007.
* [ ] **Bid Security / Bid Securing Declaration**: If bid security is required (tenders above ₦100M for goods/services or ₦300M for works), provide a certified Bank Guarantee from a commercial bank meeting exact tender validity duration (usually bid validity + 28 days). For smaller tenders, submit the exact BPP Bid Securing Declaration form signed by authorized directors.

### 4. Technical Packaging & Envelope Integrity
* [ ] **Separation of Envelopes**: Envelope A (Technical Bid) must contain **ZERO financial or pricing information**. Any mention of the total bid price in Envelope A causes immediate disqualification.
* [ ] **Pagination and Table of Contents**: Every single page in Envelope A must be sequentially numbered (1 to N) and indexed with a comprehensive Table of Contents.
