### Part 3: Deep Dive — 3 Expensive Mistakes Nigerian Companies Make When Increasing Share Capital

#### Mistake 1: Conflating Share Allotment with Share Transfer
A **Share Allotment (Form CAC 2A)** creates and issues *new* shares from the company’s unissued capital pool, expanding total registered capital and directing investment funds into company bank accounts. A **Share Transfer (Form CAC 2B)** sells *existing* shares between shareholders without altering company capital. Conflating the two leaves company balance sheets under-capitalized, triggers capital gains tax scrutiny for founders, and causes commercial banks to reject facility drawdowns.

#### Mistake 2: Paying Stamp Duty on the Total Investment Round Instead of Nominal Capital
Under Section 8 of the Stamp Duties Act, ad valorem stamp duty (0.75%) is payable **strictly on the nominal increase in share capital**, not the investment valuation or share premium. Inexperienced secretaries frequently assess stamp duty on the entire investment proceeds (e.g. paying ₦375,000 on a ₦50M round where nominal capital was only ₦100,000). This overpayment is non-refundable in practice and causes immediate CAC query rejection due to mismatched nominal ledger records.

#### Mistake 3: Overlooking the 30-Day PSC Filing Mandate (Section 119)
Under CAMA 2020, any allotment that gives an investor 5% or more equity requires a concurrent Person with Significant Control (PSC) filing. Delaying this disclosure beyond 30 days incurs compounding daily default penalties against the corporate entity and every director.
