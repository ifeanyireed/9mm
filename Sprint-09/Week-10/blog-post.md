## The Growth Trap: Why Equity Restructuring Paralyzes Nigerian Companies

In the lifecycle of every successful Nigerian enterprise, commercial expansion demands equity adjustments:
* An institutional venture investor or private equity syndicate injects $500,000 for a 20% equity stake.
* A tier-1 commercial bank requires the company to increase its paid-up share capital from ₦10,000,000 to ₦100,000,000 to qualify for an asset-backed import credit line.
* The original founders bring in strategic executive partners and need to formally allocate equity on the national corporate registry.

On paper, the business decision is celebrated in board minutes. But the moment the company secretary logs into the CAC Companies Registration Portal (CRP) to file the statutory documents, the transaction grinds to a halt.

Examiner query notices appear:
* *"QUERY: FIRS Electronic Stamp Duty Certificate amount does not reconcile with nominal increase."*
* *"QUERY: Share transfer deed attached to Return of Allotment (Form CAC 2A); filing rejected for incorrect statutory form selection."*
* *"QUERY: Failure to submit Persons with Significant Control (PSC) beneficial ownership notice for all 5%+ equity holders."*

As documented in Punch Nigeria's regulatory review (featured photo above), the Corporate Affairs Commission has eliminated manual discretion on post-incorporation filings. Every document uploaded to CRP 2.0 is subjected to algorithmic statutory cross-checks. 

When your share capital filings are queried, your corporate status locks up. You cannot download updated Certified True Copies (CTC), you cannot renew your annual statutory quad clearance (PENCOM, ITF, NSITF), and your bank relationship managers receive automated alerts preventing facility drawdowns.

Understanding the root cause requires dissecting the three fatal blunders that trip up corporate executives.

## Mistake #1: The Fatal Conflation — Share Allotment vs. Share Transfer

The most common, and legally devastating, error made by founders and general counsels is treating a **Share Allotment** and a **Share Transfer** as interchangeable corporate actions.

They are fundamentally different transactions governed by separate sections of the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020:

| Statutory Parameter | Share Allotment (Primary Issuance) | Share Transfer (Secondary Transaction) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Governing Section** | Section 149 – 154, CAMA 2020 | Section 175 – 182, CAMA 2020 |
| **Core Action** | Creating and issuing **new** unissued shares from the company’s capital pool | Transferring **already existing, issued** shares from one shareholder to another |
| **Statutory Form** | Form CAC 2A (Return of Allotment) | Form CAC 2B (Notice of Transfer of Shares) + Instrument of Transfer |
| **Impact on Total Capital** | **Increases** the company’s total issued share capital | Total issued share capital remains **completely unchanged** |
| **Recipient of Funds** | The **Company** receives investment proceeds as share premium or equity | The **Selling Shareholder** receives payment directly |
| **Stamp Duty Liability** | **Subject to 0.75% ad valorem stamp duty** to FIRS on nominal increase | Exempt from ad valorem capital duty; nominal stamp duty on instrument of transfer |
| **CAC Filing Timeline** | Mandatory within **1 month** of allotment resolution (Section 154) | File upon execution; instrument stamped within 30 days |

### The Costly Real-World Blunder
Consider a logistics company incorporated with an initial issued share capital of ₦1,000,000 divided into 1,000,000 ordinary shares of ₦1.00 each, owned 100% by Founder A. 

An investor agrees to inject ₦50,000,000 for a 20% equity stake in the company.

Many founders instruct their company secretary: *"Transfer 20% of the company to the investor, but put the ₦50,000,000 into the company’s corporate bank account for capital expansion."*

If the secretary files a **Share Transfer (Form CAC 2B)** transferring 200,000 shares from Founder A to the investor:
1. **The company's balance sheet receives ZERO equity capital**: Under Nigerian corporate law, a share transfer is a private secondary sale. The ₦50,000,000 legally belongs to Founder A personally, not the corporate entity.
2. **Tax complications arise**: Founder A may face Capital Gains Tax (CGT) scrutiny from the state internal revenue service, while the company’s audited balance sheet still shows an under-capitalized ₦1,000,000 equity base.
3. **Banking limits remain capped**: The commercial bank will reject the company's application for credit expansion because the company's official registered capital was never expanded.

Conversely, if the parties intended a primary investment, the company needed to **pass a board resolution increasing issued share capital** by creating 250,000 new shares at a premium, filing Form CAC 2A, and paying the requisite FIRS stamp duty.

Confusing these two routes results in months of administrative disentanglement and tens of thousands of Naira in wasted filing fees.

## Mistake #2: The Stamp Duty Overpayment and Remita Penalty Bleed

Under Nigerian fiscal statutes, any formal increase in a company’s share capital triggers statutory levies to two separate government authorities:
1. **Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS)**: Collects ad valorem stamp duty under Section 8 of the Stamp Duties Act (Cap S8, LFN 2004).
2. **Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)**: Collects statutory registration and filing fees on the Companies Registration Portal.

As shown in Figure 1 below (Source: Nairametrics), the integration of the FIRS TaxPro-Max platform with the CAC CRP has eliminated manual paper stamp duty stamps. Stamp duty must be assessed and generated electronically before CAC examiners will accept an allotment filing.

The second fatal mistake occurs when companies miscalculate stamp duty by confusing **Nominal Value** with **Investment Valuation (Share Premium)**:

### 1. The Ad Valorem Stamp Duty Formula
Statutory stamp duty on share capital is calculated at **0.75% of the nominal increase in share capital**:

$$\text{FIRS Stamp Duty} = \text{Nominal Increase in Capital} \times 0.0075$$

*Example*: If a company increases its nominal share capital by ₦100,000,000:
$$\text{Stamp Duty Payable} = ₦100,000,000 \times 0.0075 = ₦750,000$$

### 2. The Overpayment Disaster: Taxing the Share Premium
Suppose an angel investor values your tech company at ₦500,000,000 and agrees to invest ₦50,000,000 for 10% equity. 

To give the investor 10% equity, the company decides to issue 100,000 new ordinary shares at a **nominal value of ₦1.00 each**, issued at a **premium of ₦499.00 per share** (Total price = ₦500.00 per share × 100,000 shares = ₦50,000,000).

* **The Correct Stamp Duty**: Calculated ONLY on the nominal increase:
  $$100,000 \text{ shares} \times ₦1.00 = ₦100,000 \text{ nominal increase}$$
  $$\text{Stamp Duty} = ₦100,000 \times 0.0075 = ₦750$$
  The remaining ₦49,900,000 goes into the company's **Share Premium Account** (Section 145, CAMA 2020), which is **NOT subject to ad valorem stamp duty**.

* **The Disaster**: Inexperienced secretaries enter the total investment round (₦50,000,000) as the nominal share capital on the FIRS e-Stamp portal. FIRS generates an automated assessment of **₦375,000** (0.75% of ₦50,000,000). 
  The company pays ₦375,000 on Remita. When the document arrives at the CAC, the examiner rejects it because the nominal capital on the board resolution (₦100,000) does not match the FIRS stamp certificate (₦50,000,000). 
  **The result?** The company has overpaid FIRS by ₦374,250, and securing a refund from government consolidated revenue accounts takes 12 to 18 months of bureaucratic correspondence.

### 3. The 30-Day Penalty Trap
Under Section 154(1) of CAMA 2020:
> *"Whenever a company limited by shares makes any allotment of its shares, the company shall, within one month thereafter, deliver to the Commission for registration a return of the allotments..."*

If you delay filing Form CAC 2A beyond **30 calendar days**, the CAC portal automatically activates Section 154(2) daily late default penalties against both the corporate entity and every director. A filing that should cost ₦25,000 rapidly compounds into ₦200,000+ in accumulated default fines.

The third major operational trap is failing to execute the concurrent **Persons with Significant Control (PSC)** filing when new shares are allotted.

### What Section 119 Strictly Mandates
Whenever shares are allotted or transferred, if any individual or entity comes to hold **5% or more of the issued share capital or voting rights** (directly or indirectly):
1. The shareholder must give formal notice to the company within **7 days**.
2. The company must update its internal PSC register.
3. The company must deliver formal PSC notice to the CAC within **one month** of receipt.

### The Rejection Trigger on CRP
Most corporate secretaries still operate under CAMA 1990 habits: they file Form CAC 2A (Return of Allotment), attach the board resolution, pay the fees, and submit.

**Under CRP 2.0, this triggers an immediate algorithmic query.**
The system detects that the allotment created or modified shareholding percentages, but no matching PSC disclosure was attached.

The filing is kicked into query status. While the query lingers:
* Daily default penalties under Section 119(4) begin compounding (up to ₦25,000 daily for public entities; statutory fines for private firms).
* The company’s commercial banking mandates are flagged for "Pending Post-Incorporation Query".
* The investor cannot receive a certified share certificate or valid proof of equity ownership.

## The 4-Step Share Restructuring Execution Checklist

To ensure your equity adjustments, share allotments, and capital increases clear CAC scrutiny in under 72 hours, follow this operational checklist:

### Step 1: Convene General Meeting & Pass Exact Special Resolutions
* Draft formal Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM) minutes or unanimous written board resolutions explicitly specifying:
  * The exact nominal amount by which issued share capital is increased.
  * The classes of shares being issued (Ordinary, Preference, Non-Voting).
  * The exact allotment breakdown per subscriber (Full Legal Name, Residential Address, NIN, TIN, Phone, Email).
  * Explicit authorization for directors to sign and deliver statutory forms to the CAC.

### Step 2: Calculate and Generate Electronic FIRS Stamp Duty
* Log into FIRS TaxPro-Max / e-Stamp Duty portal.
* Calculate 0.75% strictly on the **nominal increase** in capital (exclude share premium).
* Pay the generated Remita Retrieval Reference (RRR) and download the QR-coded Electronic Stamp Duty Certificate.
* Verify that the company name, RC number, and capital amount on the FIRS certificate match the EGM resolution character-for-character.

### Step 3: Concurrently Prepare the PSC Beneficial Ownership Dossier
* For every new shareholder acquiring 5%+ equity:
  * Obtain their verified National Identity Number (NIN) or international passport (for foreign nationals).
  * Document the nature of control (voting rights, shareholding percentage, right to appoint directors).
  * Fill Form CAC-PSC01 (Notice of Person with Significant Control) alongside the allotment documents.

### Step 4: Digital Ingestion on CRP 2.0 & Status Dossier Retrieval
* Ingest the filing on the CAC portal within 14 days of EGM (well ahead of the 30-day statutory penalty cliff).
* Upload: EGM Special Resolution, Form CAC 2A, FIRS Stamp Duty Certificate, PSC Declaration, and updated Director ID credentials.
* Monitor examiner processing and retrieve the approved **Status Report**, updated **Allotment CTC**, and clean **CAC Bill of Health**.
