Federal Audit Investigation · Civil Service Compliance

Federal Ministry of Education and National Salaries Commission Unveil Nationwide SUBEB Audit: ₦24.8 Billion Lost to Ghost Teacher Payrolls

Photo: Federal Secretariat Assembly in Abuja. A multi-agency civil service forensic audit uncovered 13,800 ghost teacher profiles on state basic education payrolls. Source: Ministry of Education / NAN.

ABUJA — The Federal Ministry of Education, in formal collaboration with the National Salaries, Incomes and Wages Commission (NSIWC) and the Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC), released the executive summary of its 2026 Nationwide Basic Education Payroll Forensic Audit today in Abuja.

The comprehensive forensic examination, conducted across 1,850 public school districts in all six geopolitical zones, uncovered that ₦24.8 billion in statutory allocations was disbursed over the past 24 months to 13,800 unverified personnel listed on State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB) nominal rolls. The unverified profiles represent 8.2% of the surveyed teaching workforce.

"Public funds meant to build modern science laboratories and pay hard-working rural educators are being actively drained by organized phantom payroll cartels," stated Dr. Mahmud Bello, Director of Civil Service Audit at the NSIWC. "Our forensic teams found hundreds of instances where teachers who emigrated overseas three years ago or who retired in 2021 were still receiving monthly direct deposits because local education offices maintained manual paper muster rolls that were rubber-stamped without physical verification."

Following the publication of the findings, the Federal Government issued a binding regulatory guideline: commencing in Q1 2027, the disbursement of federal counterpart matching grants to SUBEB boards will be conditioned on states deploying real-time biometric telemetry and BVN-paired digital attendance ledgers.
