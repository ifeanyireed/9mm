## The Paper SCUML Fallacy: Why Framed Certificates Don't Protect Directors

Across Nigeria’s luxury commercial landscape, most board members sleep soundly because of a dangerous administrative illusion:
*"We obtained our SCUML certificate in 2021; it is framed in our reception lobby, so our business is completely compliant."*

In the post-MLA 2022 regulatory environment, this assumption is fatal.

The Special Control Unit Against Money Laundering (SCUML) and the Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU) no longer evaluate businesses as static paper holders. They evaluate enterprises on **continuous, automated transaction telemetry**:
1. **The 7-Day Statutory Clock**: Under Section 10 of MLA 2022, every single transaction involving cash or electronic transfers exceeding **₦5,000,000 for an individual** or **₦10,000,000 for a corporate body** must be reported to the NFIU within **seven calendar days**.
2. **Personal Director Liability**: Section 11 and Section 20 of MLA 2022 expressly strip away the traditional corporate veil. Where a DNFBP fails to report, the company faces fines starting at **₦10,000,000**, and **each individual director faces up to three years imprisonment or personal administrative fines**.
3. **The Commercial Bank PND Trigger**: Commercial banks are audited quarterly by the CBN on their DNFBP portfolios. If your corporate account processes a ₦75,000,000 property installment or luxury supercar payment, and your SCUML registration is un-synchronized or un-renewed, the bank's automated compliance bot applies an immediate **Post-No-Debit (PND)** restriction to protect the bank's operating license.

To protect your personal directorship, your company's cash flow, and your brand reputation, table these **5 executive questions** at your next board meeting.

## Question 1: "Are We Accepting Cash Installments Exceeding Section 2 Statutory Thresholds?"

The most direct route to an EFCC investigation and commercial bank account freeze is violating statutory cash payment ceilings.

Under Section 2(1) of MLA 2022:
> *"No person or body corporate shall make or accept cash payments of a sum exceeding— (a) ₦5,000,000 or its equivalent, in the case of an individual; or (b) ₦10,000,000 or its equivalent, in the case of a body corporate."*

In the real estate and automotive sectors, sales agents frequently encounter high-net-worth buyers who prefer cash deposits or split payments across multiple bank accounts to bypass scrutiny.

### The Structuring Trap:
Splitting a ₦25,000,000 land purchase into five separate cash deposits of ₦4,900,000 constitutes **"Structuring"**—a felony under Section 2(2) of MLA 2022:
* Commercial banks' automated transaction monitoring algorithms detect split deposits instantly.
* The bank is legally mandated to file a **Suspicious Transaction Report (STR)** with the NFIU without notifying the customer.
* Within 48 hours, an administrative freeze order is placed on your operating accounts while the EFCC initiates on-site compliance examinations.

As reported by Nairametrics (see Figure 1 below), the CBN and NFIU enforce zero tolerance on cash threshold circumvention across the commercial banking system.

## Question 2: "Is Our NFIU goAML Currency Transaction Reporting (CTR) Automated or Handled Manually?"

Filing Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs) on a manual spreadsheet once every few months is an invitation to regulatory disaster.

Under Section 10 of MLA 2022:
* Any single transaction or connected series of transactions exceeding **₦5,000,000 (Individual)** or **₦10,000,000 (Corporate)** must be filed on the NFIU goAML portal within **7 calendar days**.
* If your sales team closes forty luxury vehicle sales or fifteen off-plan property plots in a month, manual logging inevitably misses the 7-day deadline.
* A missed deadline generates automatic statutory late filing penalties and places the company on the NFIU high-risk non-compliance matrix.

### The Boardroom Audit Check:

## Question 3: "Do We Screen Every High-Net-Worth Buyer Against Real-Time PEP & International Sanctions Lists?"

Selling luxury assets to Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) is not illegal under Nigerian law. However, selling to a PEP without **Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)** and documented source of wealth verification is a direct statutory violation.

Under Nigerian and international AML regulations:
* **Who is a PEP?**: Current or former senior government officials, military officers, judicial officers, executives of state-owned enterprises, political party leaders, and their immediate family members and close associates.
* **The Obligation**: Before accepting funds from a PEP, the DNFBP must establish the legitimate source of funds, obtain executive compliance sign-off, and maintain an enhanced transaction monitoring audit trail.
* **The Sanctions Trap**: Selling property or luxury vehicles to individuals on the **UN, OFAC, or Nigerian Consolidated Sanctions List** results in immediate asset forfeiture, bank de-risking, and potential international criminal charges.

## Question 4: "Has Our SCUML Biometric Registration Been Updated and Audited Within the Last 12 Months?"

Under the revised operational guidelines issued by the EFCC and SCUML, the one-time paper registration era has ended.

All DNFBPs must maintain **active biometric accreditation**:
* Directorship data registered with SCUML must match CAC CAMA 2020 registers 100%.
* Authorized signatories on commercial bank accounts must possess verified biometric clearance tokens.
* The company must appoint a designated **Compliance Officer** who has undergone certified SCUML AML/CFT training and whose credentials are on file with the Commission.

If your registered directors change at CAC, but the change is not updated on the SCUML portal within 30 days:
* Your commercial bank’s quarterly mandate audit will flag a **signatory mismatch**;
* Operating accounts are placed on Post-No-Debit (PND);
* Deal closings, payroll distributions, and vendor disbursements are paralyzed.

## Question 5: "Do We Provide Documented Annual AML/CFT Training to Our Sales and Frontline Staff?"

The final question addresses the single most frequently cited deficiency during SCUML on-site examinations.

Under Section 11(2) of MLA 2022:
> *"Every designated non-financial business and profession shall... establish internal compliance programmes including the designation of compliance officers at management level, centralized information mechanisms, and continuous training for employees."*

When EFCC/SCUML compliance auditors conduct routine on-site inspections of real estate offices or auto showrooms, the first document requested after the certificate is the **Staff Compliance Training Log**:
* Did sales managers receive certified training on identifying suspicious transaction indicators?
* Did frontline cashiers understand the ₦5M / ₦10M threshold limits?
* Are training dates, attendance signatures, and syllabus modules documented?

If training logs are missing, the Commission classifies the enterprise as having a **"willful compliance failure"**, triggering administrative fines starting at ₦5,000,000 and mandatory public compliance notices.
