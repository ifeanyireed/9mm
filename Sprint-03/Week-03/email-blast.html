As documented in Punch Nigeria's coverage of the FCCPC's warning to electricity distribution companies over estimated billing and unfair charges (featured photo above), tolerance for opaque intermediary deductions and unilateral penalties is collapsing across Nigeria's commercial economy. Whether dealing with utility billing or digital retail channels, operators are scrutinising every naira siphoned off before cash hits their balance sheet.

For independent e-commerce founders selling through third-party marketplaces, the unit-economics reckoning is equally urgent. A 15% gross commission does not merely trim net profit—for a consumer brand operating on healthy 20% to 25% product margins, it consumes the majority of actual operating income while the merchant absorbs inflation in glass bottles, raw ingredients, and courier tariffs. Add doorstep pay-on-delivery rejections and two-week payout holds, and fast-growing brands frequently find themselves cash-starved despite record sales.

"Marketplaces can introduce a shopper to your product once. Paying 15% commission on that same loyal buyer's fifth bottle is a tax on your own brand equity."
► WHAT THIS MEANS FOR INDEPENDENT BRAND FOUNDERS & RETAIL CFOs:
Treat third-party marketplaces strictly as top-of-funnel discovery, never as your sole cash register. Deploy a zero-commission storefront backed by pre-funded milestone escrow so first-time shoppers trust your checkout immediately, doorstep rejections fall sharply, and settlements reach your corporate bank account upon delivery verification.
