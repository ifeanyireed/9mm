## Beyond the Paper Certificate: The Real Architecture of AML Compliance

In Week 01, we explored the sudden shock of the SCUML dragnet: property developers, luxury car dealerships, and corporate advisory firms waking up to frozen commercial bank accounts after receiving high-value customer payments.

Many business owners believe that "SCUML compliance" simply means paying an agent to obtain a paper certificate upon incorporation, framing it on the office wall, and never thinking about it again.

Under the **Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 (MLA 2022)**, this assumption is an operational catastrophe.

A SCUML registration certificate is merely an **authorization to establish compliance operations**. 

Once registered, an enterprise is statutorily classified as a reporting institution and is legally mandated to execute continuous AML/CFT controls:
* **The ₦5M / ₦10M Reporting Threshold**: Under Section 11 of the MLA 2022, every cash or electronic transaction exceeding **₦5,000,000 for individuals** or **₦10,000,000 for corporate bodies** must be reported to the Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU) within **7 consecutive days** via a statutory Currency Transaction Report (CTR).
* **The Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) Risk**: Onboarding high-net-worth public officials, senior military officers, judicial figures, or their immediate family members without enhanced Customer Due Diligence (CDD) carries severe criminal liabilities and immediate asset forfeiture risks.
* **The Mandatory Compliance Officer**: Section 10 of the MLA 2022 mandates that every DNFBP formally designate an accredited **Compliance Officer at management level** to liaise with SCUML, coordinate internal audits, and maintain the statutory AML register.

## 10 Operational Tactics for Bulletproof SCUML & AML/CFT Governance

### 1. Formally Designate a Management-Level AML Compliance Officer (`/modules/hubaml`)
Under Section 10 of the MLA 2022, compliance is not an informal task assigned to an intern. The board of directors must pass a formal resolution designating an accredited Compliance Officer at management level. The appointment letter and officer’s credentials must be formally filed with SCUML. Deploy [ComplianceHub HubAML](https://www.compliancehub.ng/onboarding?module=hubaml) to manage officer certifications and statutory reporting workflows.

### 2. Standardize Tier-3 Customer Due Diligence (CDD) & KYC Files
Eliminate casual customer onboarding. For every real estate transaction, vehicle purchase, or legal retainer, maintain a comprehensive KYC file containing:
* Certified valid government photo ID (NIN, International Passport, or Driver’s License).
* Proof of physical address (utility bill issued within the last 90 days).
* Bank Verification Number (BVN) and Tax Identification Number (TIN).
* Corporate buyers: Certificate of Incorporation, CAC Status Report, and Section 119 PSC register.

### 3. Deploy Automated Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) Screening (`/solutions`)
Under CBN and NFIU directives, transactions involving domestic or foreign PEPs require **Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)**. PEPs include current and former governors, ministers, legislators, senior military officers, heads of parastatals, and their family members. Utilize [ComplianceHub Enterprise Solutions](https://www.compliancehub.ng/solutions) to run real-time PEP and international sanctions screenings before contracts are executed.

### 4. Automate the 7-Day Currency Transaction Report (CTR) Pipeline
Under Section 11 of the MLA 2022, every single transaction exceeding ₦5,000,000 for an individual or ₦10,000,000 for a corporate entity must be logged with the NFIU within **7 days**. Build an automated trigger: the moment an inflow hits your commercial bank account or escrow ledger, the transaction details are formatted and pre-staged for NFIU digital submission.

### 5. Institutionalize Suspicious Transaction Reporting (STR) Protocols
Where a transaction appears inconsistent with a customer's known commercial profile—such as an unemployed student paying ₦80,000,000 cash for an Ikoyi penthouse or layered transfers from offshore shell entities—your compliance office must prepare a confidential **Suspicious Transaction Report (STR)**. The MLA 2022 strictly prohibits "tipping off" the customer about the report.

### 6. Standardize Board-Approved AML/CFT Policy Manuals
A primary reason DNFBPs fail SCUML regulatory audits is the absence of an institutional AML/CFT policy manual. Draft, adopt, and review annually an AML manual that outlines customer acceptance policies, risk-scoring methodologies, record retention rules, and whistleblower mechanisms.

### 7. Enforce the 5-Year Statutory Record Retention Mandate
Under Section 8 of the MLA 2022, all customer identification records, transactional registers, and reporting acknowledgments must be securely preserved for a minimum of **5 years** following the completion of the transaction or termination of the business relationship. Centralize all digital dossiers in an encrypted cloud compliance repository.

### 8. Conduct Annual Independent AML/CFT Compliance Audits
Having an internal compliance officer is not enough; the MLA 2022 requires reporting entities to undergo an annual independent AML audit by an accredited compliance consultant. The resulting audit report must identify procedural vulnerabilities, test sample transaction logs, and certify operational integrity.

### 9. Establish Pre-Emptive Commercial Bank AML Dossier Sharing
Nigerian commercial banks face heavy penalties from the CBN for processing transactions for non-compliant DNFBPs. Do not wait for your bank's compliance department to place your account on Post-No-Debit (PND). Proactively dispatch your updated SCUML certificate, compliance officer designation letter, and latest CTR acknowledgment slips to your bank branch annually.

### 10. Implement Mandatory Staff AML/CFT Training Programs (`/resources`)
Your sales directors, front-desk receptionists, and finance clerks are the first line of regulatory defense. Conduct mandatory semi-annual AML training covering cash limit prohibitions, red flags for illicit finance, customer identification protocols, and statutory reporting obligations.
