## The Anatomy of a Corporate Breakdown: How Crestview Lost Its Good Standing

For over a decade, Crestview Energy operated with the assumption that incorporation is a permanent legal status. The company had incorporated in 2014, obtained its physical certificate, and focused entirely on securing offshore charter contracts with international oil companies (IOCs).

Annual compliance was outsourced to a series of freelance corporate filing agents who operated on an ad-hoc basis.

In October 2025, the structural weaknesses in this fragmented approach triggered a catastrophic operational failure:
1. **The Remita Ghost Payment Trap**: Between 2022 and 2024, Crestview’s previous company secretary generated Remita Retrieval Reference (RRR) payment codes and collected statutory filing fees from the finance department. However, while the payment cleared on Remita, the agent **never finalized the digital submission on the CAC CRP portal**. The funds were banked, but the statutory returns were never lodged.
2. **The CAMA 2020 PSC Non-Compliance Flag**: Under Section 119 of CAMA 2020, every company must declare its Persons with Significant Control (holding 5% or more equity). Because Crestview never updated its PSC register, the CAC automated validation engine placed a "Restricted" status lock on their corporate profile.
3. **The Gazette Publication & Bank PND**: When the CAC published its national gazette of delinquent entities marked for strike-off under Section 692, Crestview’s RC number was listed. Commercial banking automated API switches detected the "Inactive" flag during a routine quarterly KYC review, slapping an automated Post-No-Debit (PND) restriction on Crestview’s primary ₦140 million operating account.

With offshore crew payroll due in 48 hours and a ₦450 million offshore pipeline support tender pending submission, Crestview was facing insolvency and commercial blacklisting.
