### Part 3: Deep Dive — The 3 Fatal Legal Drafting Traps

```text
1. The "Silicon Valley Jurisdiction" Trap
Foreign templates inevitably contain clauses declaring that all disputes will be settled under the laws of California or Delaware. In Nigeria, such clauses are legally void when applied to local consumer contracts. More critically, they alert NDPC forensic investigators that your platform has not established lawful cross-border transfer rails under Section 41–43 of the NDPA 2023.

2. Unlawful Bundled Consent (Section 26 Violation)
Standard US templates often feature a single catch-all sentence: "By using our app, you agree to our terms and the sharing of your data with advertising partners." 
Under Section 26 of the NDPA 2023, consent must be granular, specific, and unbundled. Core service functionality cannot be made conditional on accepting marketing tracking or advertising cookies. If your user signup requires a single blanket checkbox, your consent rail is legally void.

3. Absence of Mandatory Local Redress & DPO Disclosures
Under Sections 27, 32, and 48, your privacy policy must explicitly provide:
• The direct email and local address of your designated Nigerian Data Protection Officer (DPO).
• Step-by-step procedures for exercising Data Subject Access Requests (DSAR)—such as data rectification, porting, or deletion.
• Clear disclosure of the user's statutory right to lodge formal complaints directly with the Nigeria Data Protection Commission.
```
