### Part 3: Deep Dive — 3 Expensive Mistakes Luxury Merchants Make When Filing Quarterly DNFBP Returns

#### Mistake 1: Submitting Returns with Defective Customer Identification (The "Phantom Buyer" Trap)
Under Section 4 and Section 10 of MLA 2022, every DNFBP must verify customer identities using National Identity Numbers (NIN), verified BVNs, and proof of address before concluding high-value sales. Submitting quarterly returns with nicknames (e.g. *"Chief Alhaji"*), unverified phone numbers, or third-party corporate entities that do not reconcile with invoice recipients triggers immediate flags on SCUML's database, resulting in ₦5,000,000 administrative penalties for defective customer identification.

#### Mistake 2: The "Split Payment" Illusion (Structuring to Evade Cash Ceilings)
Section 2 of MLA 2022 strictly prohibits cash payments above ₦5,000,000 for individuals and ₦10,000,000 for corporate bodies. Splitting an ₦18,000,000 transaction into four separate cash installments of ₦4,500,000 to avoid filing a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) is classified as criminal structuring ("smurfing") under Section 18. NFIU artificial intelligence monitors detect these patterns automatically, attracting mandatory fines of ₦10,000,000 or 100% of the structured sum.

#### Mistake 3: Missing Annual Staff Training Logs and Internal Compliance Manuals
Under Section 11 of MLA 2022, every DNFBP must establish an internal AML program, appoint a management-level compliance officer, and document annual staff AML training. Over 75% of inspected merchants have no documented staff training registers or independent internal audit reports, leading examiners to invalidate their compliance standing during on-site inspections.
