### ACT II: TH — THE 3 FATAL PRIVACY POLICY MISTAKES (1:40 – 2:45)

**Visual**: Host stands before an interactive infographic comparing defective US templates with NDPA 2023 statutory standards.

**HOST**:
Tunde’s panic is shared by hundreds of tech founders across Lagos, Nairobi, and Accra.
During the sprint to launch an MVP, founders treat privacy policies like app store metadata—just text you paste into a URL field to pass review.

Under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, that shortcut creates multi-million Naira liabilities.

Here are the **3 fatal drafting mistakes tech startups make in their legal terms**:

**Mistake #1: The Silicon Valley Jurisdiction Trap.**
Retaining foreign choice-of-law clauses referencing California, Delaware, or English courts. In Nigeria, this does not just void your terms; under Sections 41–43 of the NDPA, it triggers immediate regulatory suspicion that your platform is unlawfully exporting citizen data to foreign servers without adequacy authorization.

**Mistake #2: Unlawful Bundled Consent (Section 26 Violation).**
Forcing users into an all-or-nothing agreement. Under Nigerian law, consent must be granular, specific, and unbundled. Core service functionality (Contractual Necessity) must be separated from promotional marketing and third-party analytics SDKs.

**Mistake #3: Omitting Statutory Local Redress & DPO Disclosures.**
Sections 27, 32, and 48 require digital platforms to explicitly publish:
- The verified direct email of an appointed Nigerian Data Protection Officer (DPO).
- Clear, programmatic mechanisms for users to exercise Data Subject Access Requests (DSARs)—including data correction and complete account erasure.
- Explicit notification of the user's statutory right to lodge complaints directly with the NDPC.
