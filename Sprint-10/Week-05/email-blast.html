In Nigerian public procurement, there is no heartbreak quite like watching your company’s multi-billion Naira federal tender package thrown out during preliminary technical opening.

Under Nigerian law, preliminary statutory evaluation is strictly pass/fail. A single expired clearance across the Statutory Quad (TCC, PENCOM, ITF, NSITF) or a financial turnover discrepancy disqualifies your entire submission under Section 16(6) of the Public Procurement Act 2007.

In this edition, we examine the three warning signs that lead to tender disqualification and deliver 10 operational fixes to guarantee 100% bid responsiveness.

LEAD PUBLIC PROCUREMENT & TENDER STORY

Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP) Reports 65% Disqualification Rate at Technical Tender Opening

Source: Punch Nigeria · Bureau of Public Procurement Stakeholder Summit in Abuja.

ABUJA — The Bureau of Public Procurement (BPP) and federal procurement directorates have revealed that over 65% of all bids submitted for federal capital projects fail at the preliminary statutory evaluation phase. As documented in Punch Nigeria’s coverage of national procurement governance assemblies (featured photo above), the overwhelming majority of disqualified contractors fail due to preventable administrative defects across the "Statutory Quad"—Tax, Pension, Industrial Training, and Employees' Compensation clearances.

"The Public Procurement Act 2007 leaves zero room for administrative discretion. Section 16(6) is clear: if an engineering firm submits a proposal with an expired PENCOM certificate, an unverified ITF clearance, or conflicting turnover figures, the evaluation committee MUST declare the bid non-responsive."
► WHAT THIS MEANS FOR CONTRACTORS & BIDDERS:

Winning public tenders requires institutional compliance discipline. Never submit a tender package without auditing your Quad certificates against the live BPP database, reconciling financial turnover across FIRS and audited statements, and verifying professional council licenses for all technical personnel.
