## The Boardroom Illusion: Assuming Your Retainer Lawyer Has It Covered

In most Nigerian corporations, board members operate under a comforting assumption:
*"We pay an external law firm or company secretarial service an annual retainer, so our CAC compliance is completely in order."*

However, empirical regulatory data reveals a starkly different reality. In a recent sector examination by the Corporate Affairs Commission, over **100,000 registered corporate entities** were earmarked for striking off the national register due to multi-year filing defaults. 

The primary cause is not intentional lawbreaking. It is the **Secretarial Black Box**:
1. **Lack of Live Telemetry**: Board meetings happen quarterly; minutes are approved; resolutions are signed. But unless a specific commercial event requires a Certified True Copy (CTC)—such as an institutional loan application or public procurement bid—no one checks the live CAC portal to confirm whether the filing was formally accepted, queried, or rejected.
2. **Hidden Queries & Accumulated Fines**: A secretarial clerk submits an annual return Form CAC 19. A minor query is raised regarding an un-reconciled share capital allotment or audited balance sheet date. The query remains unaddressed on the portal for 18 months, during which statutory late penalties accumulate daily under Section 422 of CAMA 2020.
3. **Personal Director Liability**: Under CAMA 2020 Sections 321 and 422, default penalties are not merely corporate liabilities. They attach **personally and severally** to each individual director on record.

To insulate your enterprise and its fiduciaries from unexpected regulatory paralyzation, table these **5 executive questions** at your next board meeting.

## Question 1: "What Is Our Exact Live Status on the CAC Public Search Portal Today?"

It is not enough to ask whether annual returns were filed last year. The Chairman must ask to see the live public search result on the CAC CRP database.

Under CAMA 2020, the CAC maintains three distinct administrative statuses:
* **ACTIVE**: The company is up to date with all annual return filings and mandatory disclosures.
* **INACTIVE**: The company has failed to file annual returns for two or more consecutive financial years.
* **DELISTED / STRUCK OFF**: The Commission has published the company’s name in the Federal Government Gazette and revoked its corporate charter under Section 692.

As shown in Figure 1 below (Source: Nairametrics), commercial banks operating under CBN supervisory regulations now run automated API queries against the CAC registry. The moment a corporate borrower transitions from "ACTIVE" to "INACTIVE", automated risk algorithms flag the account:

## Question 2: "Are Our Registered Directors on CAC 100% Identical to Our Boardroom Roster?"

Board composition changes regularly. Independent non-executive directors retire; private equity investors appoint representative directors; founders transition to board chair roles.

In the boardroom, these changes take effect the moment the resolution is passed. On the CAC register, however, the law is unforgiving:
* **Section 321 CAMA 2020**: Notice of any change in directors or corporate officers must be delivered to the Commission within **14 calendar days** of the change.
* **The Latency Trap**: If an associate or external law firm delays filing for 90 days, every single transaction approved by the board during that period carries legal vulnerability. Worse, statutory late penalties accumulate for every single day of delay against the company and every defaulting director.

Furthermore, if a retiring director’s name remains on the CAC portal, commercial banks and institutional lenders will continue to demand their personal KYC, BVN verification, and signatures for credit facilities, stalling corporate transactions.

### The Boardroom Audit Check:
| Boardroom Reality | CAC Registry Record | Risk Rating | Operational Consequence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 Directors (Current) | 5 Directors (Identical) | **LOW (0/10)** | Flawless banking mandates and governance standing. |
| New Director Added 30 Days Ago | Not Yet Filed on CRP | **CRITICAL (9/10)** | Accrued late penalties; actions legally subject to challenge. |
| Director Resigned 6 Months Ago | Still Listed as Active Director | **HIGH (8/10)** | Banks demand resigned director's BVN; credit facility stalled. |

## Question 3: "Have We Fully Disclosed and Certified Our Persons with Significant Control (PSC)?"

The most aggressive corporate regulatory enforcement in Nigeria today is the implementation of the **Beneficial Ownership Transparency Register** under Section 119 and Section 120 of CAMA 2020.

Under Nigerian law, any individual or entity that holds:
* 5% or more of the issued share capital;
* 5% or more of voting rights; or
* The right to appoint or remove a majority of directors,

is classified as a **Person with Significant Control (PSC)**. 

Companies are mandated to notify the CAC of PSC acquisitions, alterations, or cessations within **7 calendar days** of receipt of the disclosure, and file the statutory return within **30 days**.

As reported by TechCabal (see Figure 2 below), regulatory bodies across West Africa—including the CAC, NFIU, and Special Control Unit Against Money Laundering (SCUML)—are actively cross-referencing PSC registers against financial transactions. Failure to maintain an accurate PSC register triggers statutory penalties of up to **₦5,000,000** on the enterprise, alongside daily fines on individual directors.

## Question 4: "Are All Issued Shares Fully Paid Up and Free of the 'Unissued Share Capital' Trap?"

Under Section 124 of CAMA 2020 and the Companies Regulations 2021, the historic Nigerian corporate concept of "Authorized Share Capital" was abolished. Every Nigerian company was mandated to fully issue all unissued shares before December 31, 2022.

Despite this deadline passing, thousands of Nigerian mid-market enterprises remain in statutory breach:
* Their historical memorandum still displays Authorized Capital of ₦100,000,000, with only ₦20,000,000 issued.
* **The Legal Hazard**: Any company carrying unissued shares is barred from filing subsequent corporate returns, altering share capital, or issuing new debentures until the unissued shares are either allotted or cancelled.
* **FIRS Stamp Duty Drag**: Increasing share capital or allotting shares without automated stamp duty reconciliation on FIRS TaxPro-Max leads to immediate CAC CRP filing rejection.

### The Boardroom Audit Check:
1. Is our total issued share capital 100% equal to our registered capital?
2. Has FIRS stamp duty (0.75%) been paid and certified with an electronic stamp duty certificate on every share allotment?
3. Has the Return of Allotment (Form CAC 2A) been accepted and registered on the CRP portal with a Certified True Copy?

## Question 5: "Do We Maintain an Automated Continuous Registry Bridge or Rely on Periodic Paper Interventions?"

The final question addresses the foundational operational philosophy of corporate governance.

Historically, Nigerian corporate compliance was treated as a **periodic paper event**: once a year, an external clerk assembled paper documents, queued at the CAC office, paid bank drafts, and brought back a stamped physical receipt.

In today’s digital regulatory environment, this model is dangerously obsolete:
* Regulators conduct automated, daily digital surveillance.
* Commercial banks execute daily API status validation.
* Federal procurement portals (BPP, NIPEX, DPR) disqualify bids automatically if registry certificates show a 24-hour expiration gap.

Forward-thinking boards demand **The Continuous Board-to-Registry Bridge**:
* Direct digital synchronization between board resolution software and the CAC CRP portal.
* Real-time automated alerts 60 days before annual return deadlines (statutory deadline: 42 days after the Annual General Meeting).
* Cloud-encrypted digital archives of all Certified True Copies (CTC), certificate of incorporation, and memorandum documents, accessible to board members in one click.
