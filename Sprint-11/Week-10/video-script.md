Alhaji Kabir’s shock is shared by thousands of luxury business owners across Nigeria.
They believe having a SCUML certificate framed on their office wall makes them compliant.
It does not.

A SCUML certificate is merely an operating license.
Compliance is what you do every 90 days.

Here are the **3 fatal mistakes luxury merchants make when filing DNFBP returns**:

**Mistake #1: The Bank Verification Myth (Passing the Buck).**
Business owners assume that if money clears through a commercial bank, the buyer has already been vetted.
False!
The law requires the merchant to identify the **Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO)**. If an offshore shell company or local proxy pays for a luxury vehicle, you must obtain verified government ID and verify PEP status of the actual individual enjoying the asset.

**Mistake #2: Concealing Cash Threshold Structuring (Smurfing).**
Under Section 11 of the MLA 2022, single or connected transactions exceeding **₦5 million for an individual** or **₦10 million for a corporate body** must be formally filed as a Currency Transaction Report (CTR).
Splitting a ₦40 million car purchase into four separate ₦10 million daily transfers without a CTR is treated as criminal structuring under Nigerian anti-money laundering law.

**Mistake #3: Filing Quarterly Returns without an AML Audit Trail.**
Submitting a bare spreadsheet with buyer names and invoice totals is an open invitation for an on-site regulatory raid.
Your quarterly filing must include verified Customer Due Diligence packs, Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) screening logs, and a signed declaration from a certified AML Compliance Officer.
