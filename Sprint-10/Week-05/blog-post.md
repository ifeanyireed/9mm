## The Brutal Reality of Public Procurement: Disqualified Before the Bid is Opened

In Nigerian federal procurement, technical superiority means nothing if your statutory paperwork fails preliminary evaluation. 

Under the **Public Procurement Act 2007 (PPA 2007)**, all federal tenders are governed by strict two-stage evaluation criteria:
1. **Stage 1: Preliminary Statutory Screening**: A pass/fail administrative audit checking that the bidder satisfies all mandatory statutory obligations under Nigerian law.
2. **Stage 2: Technical & Financial Evaluation**: Detailed scoring of methodology, equipment, personnel, and commercial pricing.

Here is the operational trap that catches even seasoned contractors: **Stage 1 is non-discretionary**. Under Section 16(6) of the PPA 2007, evaluation committees are legally prohibited from evaluating the technical proposal of any contractor whose statutory clearances fail preliminary verification.

If your Pension Clearance Certificate expired three days prior to bid opening, or if your Tax Clearance Certificate turnover does not match your audited accounts, your submission is declared **"non-responsive"** on the spot. Your sealed financial bid envelope is returned unopened, and hundreds of hours of engineering preparation are completely wiped out.

## 3 Critical Warning Signs Your Tender Submission Is Heading for Disqualification

Before your bid is thrown out at public opening, your tender package almost certainly exhibits one of these three classic structural vulnerabilities:

### 1. Warning Sign #1: The Expiring Quad Trailing Trap
Every statutory clearance certificate has a distinct, desynchronized expiration date:
* **FIRS Tax Clearance Certificate (TCC)** expires December 31st annually.
* **PENCOM Pension Clearance Certificate** expires December 31st annually.
* **ITF Compliance Certificate** is valid for 12 months from issuance.
* **NSITF Compliance Certificate** is valid for 12 months from issuance.

When public sector procurement cycles drag over multiple months (common between July and November), a bidder submits a bid with a certificate that is valid on submission day, but **expires while the ministerial evaluation committee is deliberating**. Under BPP guidelines, the bidder must possess valid clearance at the exact moment of contract award. If any certificate lapses, the bid is rejected.

### 2. Warning Sign #2: The Turnover Mismatch (Audited Accounts vs. FIRS TCC vs. CAC Returns)
The second most common cause of preliminary disqualification is conflicting financial data across regulatory filings:
* Your 3-year audited financial statement reflects a 2024 turnover of **₦250,000,000**.
* Your FIRS Tax Clearance Certificate reflects a 2024 turnover of **₦180,000,000** (due to unfiled amended assessments).
* Your CAC annual return filing was declared as a "Small Company" with turnover under ₦25,000,000 to save filing fees.

When the procurement committee cross-references these documents, they flag the discrepancy as a material contradiction or fraudulent misrepresentation under Section 58 of the PPA 2007. The bid is immediately disqualified, and the company is referred to the BPP debarment committee.

### 3. Warning Sign #3: Unverified Technical Personnel & Outdated Professional Council Seals
Public tenders require certified key personnel with verifiable professional registration:
* Submitting civil engineers without current **COREN (Council for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria)** annual practicing licenses.
* Submitting quantity surveyors without active **QSRBN** or **NIQS** certification.
* Submitting architects without valid **ARCON** stamps.

Under BPP procurement guidelines, personnel CVs must be accompanied by active annual practicing seals, not just university degree certificates. Submitting unverified personnel results in automatic failure on technical qualification matrices.

### 2. Synchronize 3-Year Audited Financials with FIRS Tax Clearance Certificates
Ensure that the turnover, gross profit, and tax paid stated on your 3-year Audited Financial Statements match your FIRS Tax Clearance Certificate down to the exact kobo. If discrepancies exist due to prior tax audit reassessments, secure an official reconciliation letter from your FIRS Tax Controller prior to bid packaging.

### 3. Build an Automated 90-Day Quad Certificate Renewal Pipeline (PENCOM, ITF, NSITF)
Never wait until a major tender advert appears in the Federal Tenders Journal to renew your clearances. Establish an automated 90-day renewal cycle that initiates documentation in October, securing all new calendar-year certificates (TCC, PENCOM, ITF, NSITF) by January 15th annually.

### 4. Validate Staff Pension Remittance Schedules Against Actual Payroll Slips
Under the Pension Reform Act 2014, PENCOM clearance requires active pension contributions for a minimum of 15 employees (or 3+ for SMEs). Verify that monthly pension remittance schedules, bank payment schedules, and Pension Fund Administrator (PFA) confirmation letters reflect consistent employee headcounts and salary bands.

### 5. Secure Real-Time ITF Training Contribution Receipts and Certificate Seals
Under the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) Act, companies with 5 or more employees or ₦50,000,000+ turnover must contribute 1% of annual payroll. Settle annual contributions early in the financial year, reconcile staff headcount with ITF area offices, and verify the physical QR validation code on your ITF Compliance Certificate.

### 6. Verify NSITF Employees' Compensation Fund (ECF) Compliance and Assessment
Under the Employees’ Compensation Act 2010, employers must contribute 1% of total employee payroll to the NSITF. Maintain certified payment receipts, employee payroll registers, and formal NSITF clearance certificates to satisfy Section 16(6)(d) of the Public Procurement Act.

### 7. Audit Technical Personnel Practice Licenses Against Professional Councils
Audit all key personnel CVs included in your tender bids. Ensure that every lead engineer, surveyor, architect, and accountant possesses an active, current-year practicing license from their respective professional council (COREN, QSRBN, ARCON, ANAN, ICAN). Attach certified true copies of both degrees and annual practice seals.

### 8. Assemble Verifiable Contract Performance Records and Interim Payment Certificates (IPCs)
Procurement evaluation committees verify past project experience through documented evidence. Never submit bare completion certificates. For every cited prior project, include:
* Official Award Letter with contract sum.
* Formal Contract Agreement.
* Practical Completion Certificate or Interim Payment Certificates (IPCs) proving successful milestone execution.
