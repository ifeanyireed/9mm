## The Phantom Board: When Your Legal Reality Conflicts with the Public Register

In corporate management, internal decisions move fast. A co-founder steps down to pursue opportunities abroad. A venture capital fund invests ₦100 million and appoints a nominee director. The company relocates to a modern office tower to accommodate growing headcount.

Inside the boardroom, everyone agrees. Minutes are drafted, handshakes are exchanged, and new business cards are printed.

However, in the eyes of Nigerian law and Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) banking compliance directives, **internal agreements mean nothing until they are formally filed and approved on the CAC Company Registration Portal (CRP)**.

Under **Section 275 and Section 321 of the Companies and Allied Matters Act (CAMA 2020)**:
* A director appointment or resignation is legally ineffective against third parties until Form CAC 7A is registered.
* Any change in registered business address must be filed on Form CAC 3 within 14 days.
* Allotment of new shares must be registered on Form CAC 2A within one month, accompanied by payment of statutory stamp duties to the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS).

When these statutory filings are neglected, an enterprise enters a state of severe corporate vulnerability:
1. **The Bank Mandate Freeze**: When a newly appointed director visits the commercial bank to sign corporate account mandates, the bank queries the CAC API. If the CAC Electronic Status Report still lists the retired directors, the bank rejects the mandate and freezes the company’s accounts under anti-money laundering (AML) Know-Your-Customer rules.
2. **The Investment Dilution Block**: When institutional investors conduct legal due diligence, unrecorded share allotments or phantom equity promises halt investment rounds indefinitely.
3. **The Section 119 Beneficial Ownership Disconnect**: The CAC rejects routine annual return filings because the company's Persons with Significant Control (PSC) declarations contradict the unfiled shareholding changes.

## 10 Operational Fixes for Board and Shareholding Regularizations

### 1. Execute an Immediate CAC Status Report Reconciliation (`/modules/hubpsc`)
Do not rely on historical incorporation documents (Form CAC 2 or CAC 7) issued years ago. Log into the CAC portal or run an automated diagnostic via [ComplianceHub HubPSC](https://www.compliancehub.ng/onboarding?module=hubpsc). Download the latest CAC Electronic Status Report. Compare the names, residential addresses, shareholdings, and directorships on the registry against your active company operations.

### 2. Regularize Director Resignations Post-Haste (Form CAC 7A)
If a director has retired or resigned, obtain a formal signed resignation letter. Draft an official Board Resolution accepting the resignation. Under Section 321 of CAMA 2020, file Form CAC 7A within 14 days. Failure to file keeps the retired director legally responsible for company liabilities and prevents bank mandate updates.

### 3. File Proper Allotment of Shares & Clear Stamp Duty (Form CAC 2A)
When new equity is issued to investors or founders, internal shareholder agreements do not confer legal ownership until Form CAC 2A (Return of Allotment) is filed. Ensure that statutory stamp duty on share capital increases has been assessed and paid to the FIRS before submitting filings on the CRP portal to avoid procedural rejections.

### 4. Harmonize the Section 119 Persons with Significant Control (PSC) Register
Under Section 119 of CAMA 2020, any individual holding 5% or more equity, voting rights, or control must be registered on the national PSC database. Whenever share allotments, share transfers, or directorship changes occur, the PSC register must be updated concurrently. The CAC now programmatically halts annual returns if the PSC register contradicts shareholding filings.

### 5. Update the Registered Office Address (Form CAC 3) (`/solutions`)
Operating from an address different from the registered office on the CAC database is a statutory violation under Section 722 of CAMA 2020. Important regulatory notices, court processes, and tax assessments sent to an outdated address result in default judgments or automatic delisting notices. File Form CAC 3 using [ComplianceHub Enterprise Solutions](https://www.compliancehub.ng/solutions).

### 6. Standardize Board Resolutions with Digital Attestation
Over 45% of post-incorporation filing queries at the CAC arise from defective board resolutions. Ensure board resolutions are typed on company letterhead, clearly state the date and location of the meeting, quote the company RC number, and bear the authentic signatures of at least two active directors or one director and the accredited company secretary.

### 7. Reconcile Director BVN & NIN Records Across Commercial Banks
Nigerian commercial banks cross-check director records against the Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System (NIBSS) database. If a director’s name on the CAC Status Report differs even slightly from their Bank Verification Number (BVN) or National Identity Number (NIN) registration (e.g., maiden names, hyphenated names, or missing middle names), banks reject debit mandates. Reconcile identity records before submitting bank update files.

### 8. Regularize Cumulative Late Filing Penalties
Post-incorporation changes filed outside statutory timelines attract compounding late filing administrative penalties under the Companies Regulations 2021. Rather than abandoning overdue changes, calculate accrued penalties accurately through ComplianceHub and clear them in a single batch to restore unblemished corporate standing.

### 9. Synchronize FIRS and State Internal Revenue Service Records
Any alteration in company directorship or share capital must be notified to the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) and relevant State Internal Revenue Services (such as LIRS). Ensuring that your Tax Identification Number (TIN) profile matches your CAC record guarantees smooth issuance of annual Tax Clearance Certificates (TCC).
