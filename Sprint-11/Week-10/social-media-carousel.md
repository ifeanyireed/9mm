Under the Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 (MLA 2022), receiving customer funds via commercial bank wire does NOT exempt you from statutory Customer Due Diligence (CDD).

Commercial banks vet payments for their own institution. As a Designated Non-Financial Business and Profession (DNFBP), you are independently liable for verifying who is buying your assets and filing statutory reports.

Here are the 3 expensive mistakes that trigger SCUML regulatory queries and bank freezes:

1. Passing the KYC Buck to Commercial Banks
Merchants mistakenly believe that if an electronic bank transfer clears through NIBSS, the customer is already vetted. Under Section 6 of the MLA 2022, DNFBPs must independently verify the natural person behind every purchase. Accepting corporate vehicle purchases without identifying the Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) holding ≥5% equity is treated as facilitating anonymous money laundering.

2. Concealing Cash Threshold Structuring (Smurfing)
Under Section 11 of the MLA 2022, single or connected transactions exceeding ₦5M for individuals or ₦10M for corporate entities must be filed as a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) within 7 days. Breaking a ₦50M purchase into five ₦10M daily tranches to "stay under the radar" is illegal structuring that triggers automated red flags on the NFIU GoAML portal.

3. Submitting Bare Spreadsheets Without AML Compliance Records
Filing quarterly DNFBP returns with mere sales totals and invoices guarantees an audit query. Your quarterly filing must be accompanied by an appointed AML Compliance Officer's declaration, Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) screening records, and staff AML training logs.
