## The Lekki Luxury Developer One Signature Away From Account Freezes

On a busy Friday afternoon inside the waterfront sales pavilion of *Atlantic Crest Developments & Luxury Assets Ltd* in Lekki Phase 1, Lagos, Managing Director Mr. Femi Adesanya is closing his strongest sales week of the quarter—handling high-value transactions much like the luxury property deals captured in Punch Nigeria's coverage above.

Before 4:00 PM, three affluent clients are ready to settle immediately, and sales reps urge Finance to issue receipts before weekend bank cutoffs.

But Mr. Femi and his Chief AML Compliance Officer, Barrister Chioma Okafor, have a problem.

Reviewing the proposed settlements under the **Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 (MLA 2022)**, Barrister Chioma halts all three closings after spotting three criminal traps:

* **The ₦20M Penthouse Cash Deposit (Section 2 Strict Liability):** A buyer brings ₦20 million in bundled cash. Under Section 2(1), accepting cash above **₦5,000,000 (individual)** or **₦10,000,000 (corporate)** outside a licensed financial institution is a strict liability criminal offense.
* **The ₦35M Luxury SUV Cash Bundle:** An auto client insists on paying ₦35 million cash because his daily transfer limit maxed out—which would invite immediate SCUML/EFCC Post-No-Debit (PND) freezes across all corporate accounts if deposited piecemeal.
* **The ₦4.5M Four-Relative "Smurfing" Ambush (Sections 13 & 18):** A third buyer splits a single plot purchase into four ₦4.5 million transfers from relatives across three days to evade NFIU Currency Transaction Reporting (CTR)—illegal transaction structuring exposing both property and operating balances to Section 18 interim forfeiture.

Mr. Femi's firm has prime waterfront assets and eager affluent buyers. What it lacks is a disciplined cashless AML transaction boundary.

## 10 Ways to Eliminate Cash Exposure and Structuring Liabilities Under MLA 2022

### 1. Enforce a Board-Approved Zero-Physical-Cash Policy Across Every Showroom

Accepting a "temporary" ₦12 million cash deposit over a weekend completes a Section 2 offense the instant cash crosses your counter. As [Nairametrics' November 9, 2026 coverage of Federal High Court property forfeiture proceedings](https://nairametrics.com/2026/11/09/court-reverses-interim-forfeiture-of-ex-warri-refinery-mds-properties-faults-efccs-evidence/) (courthouse pictured above) demonstrates, clean evidentiary trails decide property forfeiture outcomes in court.

Pass a zero-exception Board Resolution prohibiting physical cash sales, post "Cashless Premises — MLA 2022 Compliant" signage, and accept only bank RTGS, NIP wires, or verified drafts.

**Why it matters:** Removes Section 2 strict liability criminal exposure from your sales floor and directors completely.

### 2. Deploy Automated Structuring ("Smurfing") Detection Across Cumulative Receipts

Buyers dodging NFIU reporting rarely send one ₦18 million transfer; they split four ₦4.5 million wires across relatives over three days.

Configure your ledger to aggregate all incoming funds tied to one unit, VIN, or buyer across rolling 7-, 14-, and 30-day windows—triggering an automatic hold whenever cumulative tranches cross ₦5 million (individual) or ₦10 million (corporate).

**Why it matters:** Stops Section 13 transaction structuring before your firm becomes an unwitting accessory.

### 3. Enforce Strict Same-Name Beneficiary Verification

Accepting funds from an unrelated cousin, aide, or shell company for a property titled in another name invites immediate PND holds and Section 18 disputes.

Mandate a **Contract-to-Sender Matching Rule**: the sending account name must match the verified buyer on the Offer Letter, or carry a notarized Third-Party Funding Authorization plus full KYC on the actual funder pre-settlement.

**Why it matters:** Preserves an unbroken, court-defensible record proving who funded every asset purchase.

### 4. Mandate Tiered BVN, NIN, and Beneficial Ownership KYC Pre-Invoice

Collecting ₦50 million deposits first and chasing IDs at handover commingles tainted funds inside operating accounts before red flags emerge.

Gate every Offer Letter behind digital BVN, NIN, government ID, verified utility address, and—for corporate buyers—a live CAC Status Report identifying every 5%+ Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO).

**Why it matters:** Filters out high-risk counterparties before a single Naira enters your corporate bank accounts.

### 5. Automate Real-Time Politically Exposed Person (PEP) and Sanctions Screening

Selling to Politically Exposed Persons is legal, but doing so without Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) and documented Source of Wealth is a severe SCUML violation—which is why fast-scaling Abuja and Lagos developers (illustrated in Punch Nigeria's coverage above) embed automated screening into buyer onboarding.

Screen every buyer and UBO automatically against domestic PEP lists, EFCC watchlists, and global sanctions databases, routing matches straight to your AML Officer for logged EDD approval.

**Why it matters:** Lets you close legitimate high-net-worth PEP deals safely with airtight inspection documentation.

### 6. Automate Weekly SCUML Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs)

DNFBPs must file statutory **Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs)** and Cash-Based Transaction Reports (CBTRs) within seven days for reportable transactions exceeding ₦5 million (individual) or ₦10 million (corporate).

Use an automated compliance register that flags reportable settlements upon bank credit and prepares ready-to-file SCUML/goAML XML returns well inside the 7-day window.

**Why it matters:** Eliminates administrative SCUML default fines and demonstrates active regulatory cooperation.

### 7. Establish a Confidential 24-Hour STR Escalation Workflow (Zero Tipping Off)

If a buyer refuses BVN verification, demands ₦35M cash acceptance, or asks how to split payments below ₦5M, MLA 2022 mandates filing a confidential **Suspicious Transaction Report (STR)** with NFIU within 24 hours without alerting ("tipping off") the client.

Place a discreet escalation trigger inside your CRM routing alerts silently to your Chief AML Officer for timely 24-hour filing.

**Why it matters:** Protects executives from criminal tipping-off charges and proves good-faith compliance.

### 8. Ring-Fence Client Property Deposits in Dedicated Project Escrow Accounts

Mixing subscriber deposits into general corporate accounts means one tainted buyer triggering a PND freeze paralyzes payroll, contractors, and unrelated estate phases at once.

Isolate incoming deposits in dedicated project escrow accounts with licensed banks, releasing funds to operations only after KYC, PEP, and source-of-funds sign-off clears green.

**Why it matters:** Contains legal exposure to a single transaction instead of freezing your entire enterprise.

### 9. Keep Your SCUML Certificate and Annual AML Risk Assessment Current

Outdated SCUML filings—especially after adding branches, changing directors, or expanding into auto trading—trigger abrupt bank restrictions under CBN/SCUML rules.

Update your SCUML certificate, Compliance Officer designation, and Enterprise AML/CFT Risk Assessment annually inside a vault accessible to relationship bankers immediately.

**Why it matters:** Prevents sudden KYC-driven corporate bank account blocks.

### 10. Conduct Quarterly AML Training and Gate Sales Commissions on Compliance Sign-Off

Written policies fail if commission-driven sales agents coach clients to split wires below ₦5 million to hit monthly targets.

Deliver quarterly staff AML refreshers and release sales commissions only after the AML Officer signs off complete KYC, same-name funding, and SCUML reporting on the file.

**Why it matters:** Aligns sales-floor incentives with MLA 2022 compliance rather than pitting revenue against survival.
